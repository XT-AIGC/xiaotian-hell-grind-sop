# JKD Core — Principles, Stance, Footwork, and Ranges

The core of the style. Everything written here must read ON SCREEN — each principle has a visual signature.

## Philosophy → What It Looks Like on Screen

| Principle | Essence | Visual signature in the frame |
|---|---|---|
| **Interception** | Do not block and wait — strike AT THE MOMENT the opponent attacks. Jeet Kune Do = "the way of the intercepting fist" | The opponent begins a strike → the hero has already landed first; the opponent's strike dies halfway |
| **Economy of motion** | The shortest line, zero unnecessary movement | No sweeping arcs; straight lines, short paths, victory in 2–4 actions |
| **Non-telegraphed** | A strike without a windup or a cue from the shoulder, hip, or face | The fist launches from guard WITHOUT drawing back; the facial expression does not change before impact |
| **Longest weapon → nearest target** | The longest weapon goes to the nearest target | Lead hand to the face, lead foot to the knee/shin — not the rear hand travelling across half the body |
| **Simultaneous attack & defense (lin sil die dar)** | Defense and strike are one movement | One hand redirects the attack while the other lands simultaneously; not “block, THEN strike” |
| **Broken rhythm** | Uneven tempo, an attack on the half-beat | An explosion of speed → complete stillness → an explosion; the opponent misses during the pause |
| **Be like water** | Adaptation, no fixed form | The hero does not repeat the same combination; he flows between ranges |

## Stance — Bai-Jong (On-Guard Position)

The MOST RECOGNIZABLE signature of the style. Describe it explicitly in the prompt:

- **Strong side FORWARD** (for Bruce Lee — right hand and right foot forward). This distinguishes JKD from boxing.
- Body sideways/half-sideways to the opponent (a narrow silhouette), NOT square-on.
- Feet slightly wider than shoulder width, rear knee spring-loaded, rear heel lifted — a “fencer's” posture.
- Light weight on the balls of the feet, with a slight spring.
- Lead hand half-extended at shoulder level, rear hand near the chin/chest.
- Chin down, eyes on the opponent over the lead shoulder.

Prompt phrase: `narrow sideways right-lead stance, rear heel raised, lead hand half-extended, weight bouncing lightly on the balls of his feet`

## Footwork

- **Push shuffle** — a short pushing step forward/backward; the feet do not cross and the stance is maintained. The foundation of entering an attack.
- **Pendulum step** — a pendulum: the lead foot retracts into the rear foot's place and immediately returns; evading a low kick + an instant counterattack.
- **Circling** — moving in an arc away from the opponent's strong hand with small sliding steps.
- **Burst (quick advance)** — an explosive, deep entry covering 1–1.5 m in one beat, usually together with a straight lead.
- **Curving/sidestep** — leaving the attack line while striking simultaneously.

On screen, the footwork is light, springy, “fencing-like” — not flat-footed shuffling.

## Four Ranges

1. **Kicking range** — long: low kicks to the knee/shin (jeet tek), oblique kick, side kick to the body.
2. **Punching range** — medium: straight lead, finger jab, lead hook, backfist.
3. **Trapping range** — close: pak sao / lop sao, immobilizing the opponent's arms + headbutts through the open line, elbows.
4. **Grappling range** — clinch/ground: JKD aims NOT to linger here; throw/sweep and exit.

Classic scene progression: enter through kicking → punching → trapping, finish in 2–3 beats.

## Five Ways of Attack

1. **SDA (Simple Direct Attack)** — a single direct strike without preparation: a straight lead out of nowhere.
2. **ABC (Attack By Combination)** — a short 2–3-strike combination: jab → cross → hook low.
3. **PIA (Progressive Indirect Attack)** — feint on one line, strike on another: show the face attack → hit the body.
4. **HIA/ABD (Attack By Drawing)** — bait: leave an opening, the opponent attacks → intercept (stop-hit).
5. **HIA (Hand Immobilization Attack)** — trapping: pin/remove the opponent's hand and strike through the open line.

For scene drama: a master hero most often wins through ABD (intercepting a provoked attack) — the most “intelligent” and cinematic approach.

## Scene Archetypes

- **JKD master**: calm face, minimal movement, almost motionless between bursts, wins through interceptions. From book 5: behaves “as in ordinary everyday life” — no bulging eyes, zero rage; a “diffuse” peripheral gaze rather than staring at the opponent's hands. The rear heel is a “cocked trigger.”
- **Student**: the same arsenal, but a more even tempo, more ABC combinations, fewer stop-hits.
- **Opponent (non-JKD)**: sweeping haymakers, a square stance, telegraphed movement — the contrast emphasizes the hero's style. Book 1 (sources/03) contains a catalog of ~15 “incorrect” stances: ready-made poses for opponents.
- **Early style / classical school** (sources/08): weight 70/30 on the rear leg, vertical fist from the center of the chest, Wing Chun structure — for “young fighter” scenes or a contrast between schools.

## Feints and Timing (from sources/05, 07)

- No more than two consecutive feints; the first feint “eats up” half the distance.
- The “show → strike” pair: feint low → jab to the eyes; show the face attack → strike the body. The feint must make the opponent VISIBLY react (a hand twitches into defense) — this is a separate micro-beat.
- Anger and fear slow a fighter down — an angry opponent becomes even more telegraphed.

## Camera (Lee's Own Cinematographic Principle, Book 5)

For the decisive exchange — a long, fixed, distant shot instead of a montage of cuts: the speed and precision of the technique must read in full, without “rescue” editing. Lee's 3–4-phase photo sequences (sources/07) are ready-made storyboard templates for individual techniques.
