# MOVES CATALOG v2 — Canonical JKD Movement Dictionary (Seedance-proof)

Purpose: Victor names a movement using ANY alias — the skill resolves it to the English snippet below and incorporates it into the prompt, adapting it to the character (gender, lead hand), opponent, camera, and shot size.

## WRITING LAWS (Rewritten Following ULTRALORDGEN, v1.4)

1. **Do NOT put terminology in the prompt body.** “Bai-jong,” “pak sao,” and “jeet tek” are noise with unrelated priors for the model: it does not know the terms and produces default movie kung fu. Only body geometry goes into the prompt: which limb, from where, along which path, to where, and what happens to the target. Terms belong in Victor's choreography breakdown and in this catalog as lookup keys.
2. **Zero prohibitions.** Naming something in a prohibition summons it (“no high kicks” produces a high kick; “zero windup” produces a windup). All locks are affirmative: “every kick stays below belt height,” “the first visible motion of the arm IS the strike already travelling.”
3. **States, not transitions.** Describe the strike already travelling + the physics of its consequences (head snaps back, body folds). Do not write preparation (“draws back,” “gets ready”) at all.
4. **Every strike carries the target's reaction** — without explicit physics, the model “misses” the contact.
5. **Stance belongs in the first frame**, with full geometry (entry S1); in later frames, use the short reprise “the narrow side-on stance” (identical words, the law of redundancy).
6. **An uncommon strike relies on three layers** (the law that “an image anchor is stronger than a description”): (a) a familiar visual anchor the model has certainly seen in its data — backhand slap, tennis backhand, mule kick, stamping a door shut, hammering a nail; (b) an explicit striking surface — which part of the fist/foot touches the target FIRST, where the palm and fingers point; (c) the target's reaction agrees with the strike's VECTOR — a backfist sends the head SIDEWAYS, a straight punch sends it backward, a side kick sends the body straight back. A mismatched reaction “retrains” the model back into an ordinary punch.

**Global style lock** (at the end of every fight prompt, using identical wording):
`FIGHT STYLE LOCK: every kick stays below belt height; every punch travels a short straight line launched from where the hands already rest at guard — the first visible motion of a limb IS the strike itself; between exchanges the fighter is completely still in his narrow side-on stance; the fight is short straight lines and long stillness.`

## S — Stances and Behavior

**S1. Bai-jong (Fighting Stance)** — stance, on-guard, combat stance
`He stands almost side-on to his opponent, right shoulder and right foot leading, feet just past shoulder width, rear heel floating a finger's width off the ground, knees softly bent, weight riding the balls of both feet with a light constant spring. His lead hand floats half-extended at shoulder height, rear fist rests at his chin, elbows close to the ribs, chin tucked, eyes steady over the lead shoulder, face calm and unreadable.`

**S2. Concealed Stance** — conciliatory posture, “I don't want to fight”
`His palms hover half-raised in front of his chest as if calming the man down — but his body has already bladed half-sideways, right foot slid ahead, knees soft, weight on the balls of his feet.`

**S3. Stop-Still (Broken Rhythm)** — pause, freeze, stillness
`He settles back into the narrow side-on stance and goes completely still — feet planted, hands frozen at guard, only his eyes tracking over the lead shoulder. The stillness holds a full second.`

**S4. Early Stance (Classical School)** — young Bruce, 70/30
`He sinks back onto his rear leg — seven tenths of his weight on it — torso nearly square, one fist held at the center of his chest with knuckles vertical and thumb up, forearm riding an invisible line down the middle of his body.`

## F — Footwork

**F1. Push shuffle** — advancing shuffle, shuffle
`His rear foot shoves the ground and the whole stance glides forward one short step, front foot skimming the floor, feet keeping their spacing, the side-on shape unchanged.`

**F2. Step & slide** — step and draw the rear foot up
`He steps and slides forward, front foot then rear, closing distance patiently, stance intact.`

**F3. Pendulum** — pendulum, retracting the leg
`His lead leg whips back beside the rear foot and the opponent's low kick sweeps through empty air — in the same breath the lead leg swings forward again, carrying him straight into his counter.`

**F4. Burst** — explosive entry, surge
`From dead stillness he covers a full meter in one explosive gliding step — like a fencer's lunge — torso staying level, arriving inside striking range within a single beat.`

**F5. Circling / sidestep** — circling in an arc, leaving the line
`He drifts along a curve around the opponent on small quick fencing steps, always facing him.` / `A half-step carries him off the attack line at the last instant — the punch passes a palm's width from his cheek while his feet stay planted under him.`

**F6. Hovering** — floating, springing lightly
`He hovers on the balls of his feet with a light constant spring in the knees, head gliding at one steady height — coiled, charged, almost motionless.`

## H — Hands

**H1. Straight lead** — lead straight, strike #1
`His lead fist fires from exactly where it hovers at guard — one straight line to the opponent's chin, knuckles vertical, thumb up, elbow unfolding behind the fist, a short glide-step feeding body weight into it. The head snaps back hard; his hand is already back at guard while the man is still staggering.`
Reaction: head snaps back, two steps backward / sinking down.

**H2. Finger jab (bil jee)** — jab to the eyes
`His lead hand spears out fingers-first — an OPEN hand, fingers straight, pressed together and stiff, fingertips striking the attacker's eyes like a spear tip — the longest, fastest reach in the fight. The man flinches back, both hands flying to his face, eyes screwed shut.`

**H3. Lead hook** — hook with the lead hand
`Off the straight punch his lead arm bends and hooks tight into the side of the jaw, elbow staying down, torso turning only a few degrees — a short compact arc. The head whips sideways, knees dipping.`

**H4. Backfist** — basic backfist
`His arm whips out and across in a BACKHAND arc — the same motion as a backhand slap but with a closed fist — the BACK of his knuckles, the side where a watch face sits, cracking across the temple, palm turned down and toward himself. The head jerks SIDEWAYS with the blow; the arm recoils along the same arc.`

**H5. Snapping backfist** — whipping wrist snap
`From guard his hand whips across in a short backhand snap — knuckle side of the fist leading, palm turned down — the back of the knuckles cracking across the nose like flicking water off the fingertips, back at guard inside the same beat. The head flicks aside, eyes watering.`

**H6. Heavy backfist** — “club,” finisher
`His whole torso torques into a wide BACKHAND swing — a tennis backhand thrown with a closed fist — the back of the knuckles arriving across the jaw like a club. The man's head whips sideways and he spins a quarter turn, dropping.`

**H7. Straight blast** — chain straight punches along the center
`Both fists take turns pistoning down one straight rail at the center of the opponent's chest and face — vertical knuckles, thumbs up, elbows brushing his own ribs, the hands cycling like bicycle pedals, three hits a second — while his feet chew forward in tiny steps. The man back-pedals, arms flailing, and topples onto his back.`

**H8. One-inch punch** — inch punch (on request)
`His fist starts already touching the man's chest — then hip, shoulder and wrist snap once, together, and the man launches off his feet backward into the shelving.`

**H9. Shovel hook** — “shovel” to the liver
`A short scooping hook digs up under the floating ribs from a palm's width away, knuckles half-turned up. The man folds sideways around the spot.`

**H10. Cross** — rear straight
`His rear fist drives straight through behind the lead punch, hips snapping over, landing on the line the lead just opened.`

**H11. Hammer fist** — hammer
`His fist rises and drops like a hammer driving a nail — the PINKY side of the clenched fist, the soft bottom edge, landing on the back of the bowed neck.`

**H12. Elbow** — elbow strike
`Chest to chest, his arm folds and the point of the elbow slices a short flat arc across the jaw. The head snaps aside, the man reeling into the wall.`

## K — Legs (All Below the Belt by Default)

**K1. Side Kick to the Knee (Jeet Tek)** — stop-kick to the knee
`His torso tilts away as the lead knee lifts and the leg pistons out SIDEWAYS — hip rolled over, the OUTER EDGE and heel of the sole leading, toes pointing across the frame — stamping into the side of the opponent's forward knee, the whole kick living below knee height, like kicking a low door shut. The joint folds and the man pitches forward.`

**K2. Side Kick to the Body** — “launcher,” finisher
`He glides in, torso tilting back as the lead knee folds up toward his chest and the leg pistons out SIDEWAYS at waist height — HEEL leading, toes across the frame, hip and shoulder stacked in one straight brace from shoulder to heel — slamming into the sternum like stamping a heavy door open. The man leaves his feet and flies two-three meters straight back into the crates, folding around the impact.`

**K3. Oblique kick** — stomping kick to the shin
`His lead foot stamps down and forward across the opponent's shin, scraping bone and crushing the instep. The leg buckles under him.`

**K4. Hook kick** — low round kick
`His lead leg snaps a short curving kick into the ribs at belt height — the TOP of the foot and lower shin landing flat against the body — the leg whipping in and back out along one arc, hip lifting only a few degrees. The man folds over the spot, an arm clamping his side.`

**K5. Jeet juk tek** — intercepting stop-kick
`The attacker's front foot has barely left the ground when the hero's lead leg is already pistoning out sideways — heel leading, torso tilted away — the sole meeting the advancing knee mid-step. The attack collapses before it forms, the attacker's own momentum driving him onto the heel.`

**K6. Leg cut** — cutting the supporting leg beneath a round kick
`As the opponent's round kick rises, the hero drops low and his shin chops into the standing leg at calf height — the base sweeps out and the man crashes flat on his back, the kick dying in the air.`

**K7. Knee** — knee strike from a hold
`Both hands clamp the back of the attacker's neck and drag it down onto the rising knee; knee meets solar plexus and the man crumples to all fours, mouth gaping for air.`

**K8. Spin back kick** — spinning rear kick (on request)
`He pivots on the lead foot, his back turning to the opponent for half a beat, and the rear HEEL drives STRAIGHT BACK like a mule kick into the midsection — a piston along one line, one clean rotation, the foot landing already facing the opponent again.`

## T — Trapping and Interceptions

**T1. Pak sao** — slap-deflection + strike in one beat
`His rear palm slaps the attacker's guarding arm down and inward, pinning it — and in the same instant, inside the same beat, his lead fist shoots through the opened line into the jaw. One motion, both hands working at once. The head snaps back, the pinned arm still trapped low.`

**T2. Lop sao** — wrist pull + meeting strike
`His hand closes on the attacker's wrist and yanks it down past his own hip — elbow slamming to his ribs, hips turning into the pull — so the man lurches face-first into the short punch already travelling the other way. Pull and hit land as one.`
Reaction: body is jerked forward and down, falling past the hero.

**T3. Jao sao** — flowing around a block
`His striking hand meets the block and stays glued forward, sliding around it in a small tight circle, and continues into the face along the new open line — one flowing unbroken path. The head jerks back off the sneaking hit.`

**T4. Jut sao** — jerking the guard down
`His fingers hook the top of the opponent's forearm and jerk it down a hand's width — for half a beat the face stands open, and the other fist takes it.`

**T5. Stop-hit** — interception with a strike (the style's DNA)
`The attacker's shoulder just begins to load its swing — and in that exact instant the hero's lead fist is already landing on his chin, the straight line beating the curve. The swing dies halfway, the attacker's own forward weight doubling the impact.`

**T6. Parry + hit** — deflection and strike in one beat
`His rear palm pushes the incoming punch a hand's width off line while his lead fist travels the shorter straight path and lands first — deflection and strike inside one single beat.`

**T7. Chi sao** — sticky hands
`Their forearms stay glued at the wrists, rolling in tight circles, weight trading back and forth — then he explodes through the contact, a vertical fist bursting from inside the roll into the chest.`

**T8. Trap Series (Gates)** — chain of traps
`Three hand-fights inside two beats: his palm slaps the guard down, his other hand snatches the wrist and yanks, and the freed fist cracks across the temple — slap, snatch, strike, each move opening the door for the next.`

## G — Holds, Throws, and Groundwork

**G1. Takedown Through Lop Sao** — pulling down to the ground
`The yank on the wrist meets the man's own stumble — the hero's foot sweeps the lead ankle and he crashes past, face-down onto the asphalt, arm still trailing in the hero's grip for a beat.`

**G2. Armbar** — standing elbow lever
`He feeds the punching arm across his own forearm and levers it straight, locking the elbow against the joint; the man folds down to his knees, arm trapped rigid.`

**G3. Escaping a Hold** — striking a “low-cost” target
`Instead of wrestling the grip, his heel stamps flat onto the grabbing hand — the fingers spring open.`

## W — Weapons

**W1. Against a Knife** — disarming
`He angles off the knife's line with one sharp step and his lead foot stabs low into the knifeman's forward knee — the lunge collapses mid-thrust. Both his hands snap onto the weapon wrist, his elbow drops onto the trapped forearm, and the knife falls from sprung-open fingers, skittering across the wet asphalt. He shoves the man away and resets side-on, still.`

**W2. Against a Stick/Bat** — entering inside the swing
`He steps INSIDE the swing before it gathers speed, forearm jamming the attacker's arm at the bicep, and his fist lands in the same motion — the club's arc dying behind his shoulder.`

## D — Training Scenes

**D1. Pads** — focus mitts
`Focus mitts crack in fast pairs — the pad man feeding angles, vertical-knuckle punches snapping in and recoiling, feet gliding between combinations.`

**D2. Wooden dummy** — wooden training dummy
`His forearms clatter in circles around the wooden dummy's three arms — palms slapping, wrists hooking, a low kick thudding into the post leg — a fast rhythmic drum of wood and skin.`

**D3. Isometrics** — straps, static holds
`He strains against a fixed leather strap, the punch frozen at half extension, arms trembling, tendons ridged along the forearms — three long seconds, then release and a long exhale.`

**D4. Clap Drill** — exploding on a signal
`He stands loose, eyes soft — at the clap of hands he detonates forward, one punch, and is still again before the echo fades.`

**D5. Bruce's Push-Ups** — on two fingers
`Push-ups on two fingers and a thumb of each hand, body one rigid plank, lowering slow and trembling with control.`

**D6. Shadowboxing** — fighting an imaginary opponent
`He shadowboxes in bursts — three blurred strikes, a frozen half-second, a glide sideways, one more — the rhythm broken and unpredictable, breath hissing with each hit.`

5. FIGHT STYLE LOCK — at the end of every fight prompt (see the top of this file), using identical wording.
7. Put the sound of every impact into the prompt's closed AUDIO list; otherwise the strikes are silent.
