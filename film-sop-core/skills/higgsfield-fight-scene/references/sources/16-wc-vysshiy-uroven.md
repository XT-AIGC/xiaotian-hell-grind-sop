# 16 — Wing Chun: Advanced-Level Techniques

**Type:** book (scan, 71 pp.) — Liu Weihan, “Wing Chun. Advanced-Level Techniques” (third book in the series), Sport-Press, 2003.

**What it adds:**
- Third, highest form, Biu Jee Tao (“striking fingers form”), through photo stages: spear fingers, diagonal elbows, wrist rotations.
- Complete Muk Yan Jong Fa wooden-dummy sequence (116 movements) — ready close-combat combinations of “block+strike on one beat.”
- 28 defense-counterattack combat techniques with target points (face/throat/torso/knee) — effectively storyboards for fight beats.
- New advanced striking elements: Kup Jarn elbow, Jut Sau palm edge, Wun Jeui palm heel.
- Principles: centerline, simultaneous attack and defense, close range, economy of movement, Yin-Yang (soft overcomes hard).

## Advanced principles (for scene physics)
1. **Centerline:** protect your own, attack the opponent's; strikes follow the shortest straight path into the median plane.
2. **Simultaneity:** both hands work at once — one blocks/restrains, the other strikes on the same beat. No “block, then strike” phase.
3. **Economy:** minimum movement between defense and attack; no windups or broad trajectories.
4. **Sticky hands:** constant forearm contact, sensing the opponent's pressure (Chi Sao skill); fighting is with hands, kicks less frequent, to knee and groin.
5. **Close range:** fighter closes the gap and works point-blank; elbows enter at maximum proximity.
6. **Power release (Biu Jee):** hand and arm relaxed throughout, muscles tense only in the final fraction of a second — a “penetrating,” whipping strike. Trained by piercing a sheet of paper with the fingers.
7. **Pulling toward oneself:** pull the opponent's captured arm into your strike — “adding velocities,” multiplying impact force.
8. **Yin-Yang:** soft evasion/body rotation against a hard attack, deflection into emptiness along a tangent; a counterattack is always embedded in the block (Yang dot within Yin).

## Hand strikes (Cantonese names)
- **Yat Chi Kuen** — straight vertical-fist punch along the centerline; striking surface — lower three knuckles.
- **Side punch** — horizontal arc to temple or lower jaw; rare, situational.
- **Jau Kuen** — straight rising fist (uppercut equivalent), from below forward-upward to chin and solar plexus; usually the second strike in a counterattack.
- **Wun Jeui** — palm-heel strike; straight to head (concussion) or rising to chin; to torso — liver, heart.
- **Biu Jee** — fingertip strike (middle, ring, index fingers); hand horizontal or vertical, arm relaxed until the final instant; targets — eyes, throat, solar plexus. Only in exceptional cases. “Phoenix Eye” variation — fist with an extended middle-finger phalanx to facial points.
- **Jut Sau** — chopping palm-edge strike, elbow extension along a horizontal arc; outer hand edge; primary target — throat; strengthened by pulling the captured arm toward oneself.
- **Kup Jarn** — diagonal forward-downward elbow strike along a descending arc; close-combat weapon, strengthened by torso rotation and forward lean; targets — throat, head, collarbone; usually the second/third strike in a combination.
- **Gwai Jarn** — horizontal elbow strike (from the previous level, used in head-strike combinations).

## Kicks
- **Straight kick** — pushing kick to lower torso; or heel/sole horizontally; in counterattack — knee, groin, lower abdomen.
- **Side kick** — with foot edge, usually to stop an advance; in counterattack — outside of knee joint (ligament tearing, disabling the support).
- **Knee** — to groin and torso in clinch/neck grip.
- Legs always target the lower level; foot quickly returns to ground after striking.

## Defensive techniques
- **Guzn Sau** — double block comprising two movements: one arm Gan Sau, the other Jim Sau; against straight and circular mid-level kicks; strengthened by torso rotation.
- **Fook Sau** — deflecting block with inner forearm, against upper- and middle-zone attacks.
- **Tan Sau** — inside-outward palm-up deflection, arm rotating along its longitudinal axis; usually with simultaneous hand or leg counterattack.
- **Lap Sau** — restraining grab of the opponent's arm with one/both hands; after grabbing — pull toward yourself/downward to strengthen the answering strike.
- **Bong Sau** — “wing”: block with outer forearm edge deflecting the attack upward and sideways; creates entry for counterattack, often with a simultaneous kick.
- **Pak Sau** — slapping block with inside of hand horizontally toward opposite shoulder, with simultaneous counterattack from other hand.
- **Gum Sau** — downward pressing palm-heel block, against middle/lower-level attacks; also restrains opponent's arms.
- **Fut Sau** — block with outer forearm edge upward from below with arm rotation, against straight head attacks.
- **Gan Sau** — chopping block with outer forearm edge along a downward arc, deflecting the attacking limb from inside outward.
- **Wu Sau** — standby backup palm before chest (second hand's ready position).
- **Huen Sau** — circular wrist rotation (clockwise/counterclockwise) — transition between contacts, changing line.

## Biu Jee Tao form (third, highest)
- Essence: “striking fingers form” — attacking concept; the main component of victory is attack (unlike Siu Lim Tao and Chum Kiu). Teaches close-combat tactics, finger strikes, Kup Jarn elbows, circular sweeping leg movements (knocking the opponent down in counterattack), maximum use of potential.
- Movements fast and smooth, carrying “explosive” power without visible tension.
- Phase-by-phase skeleton (figs. 68–268): frontal stance (toes inward) → Biu Jee series forward-upward/downward/right-left from centerline with Huen Sau rotations → Yat Chi Kuen strikes → 90°/180° turns with diagonal Kup Jarn elbows (left/right, series of 2–3) → Biu Jee + Wu Sau/Gan Sau/Tan Sau combinations → double Guzn Sau blocks with hand changes on 45°/90° turns → Fut Sau with 60° head and body turns → Jut Sau palm-edge strikes + Wun Jeui to chin → deep forward bends with arms lowered and spread sideways (recovery/rooting) → final Yat Chi Kuen, Jau Kuen, Huen Sau, fists close, exhalation.
- Cinematic form markers: a fan of fast stationary finger thrusts, sharp 90–180° angle changes with an elbow strike in the new direction, wrist rotations as “reloading,” low bow/bend with an arm wave.

## Muk Yan Jong Fa (wooden dummy)
- 116-movement dummy sequence (150–170 cm cylinder, two upper “arms” + one “lower arm,” bar “leg”). Develops limb conditioning, entry precision, seamless block-strike without risk of injuring a partner, impressive “breaking through” defense.
- Typical modules (all — “block on dummy arm + body strike on one beat”):
  - Lap Sau (grab the “arm”) + Wun Jeui palm heel to chin/ribs/floating ribs.
  - Tan Sau with a stepping placement of the foot by the dummy's “leg” + Wun Jeui; followed by Guzn Sau.
  - Bong Sau pressing the “arm” from outside + transition to two-hand Yan Jeung/Wun Jeui.
  - Pak Sau on the “arm” + Yat Chi Kuen to solar plexus; left/right Pak Sau series changing sides.
  - Fook Sau + Jut Sau palm edge to throat/neck.
  - Kuin Sau/Gum Sau — downward parries with upward palm strikes to the “arms.”
  - Foot strikes to dummy “knee” + arcing steps right/left with foot placement behind the “leg” (stepping behind the support).
  - Side kick to knee, without lowering leg — upper-level hand series.
- Choreographic pattern: fighter circles dummy with arcing steps, every angle change = new block-strike; hands clatter against bars in fast staccato.

## Defense and counterattack technique (28 combat scenarios)
- Logic of all techniques: opponent steps and throws straight fist/kick → instant neutralization (Pak/Bong/Tan/Guzn/Gan/Fut Sau) → Lap Sau grab pulling toward oneself → 2–4 consecutive point strikes → elbow/kick/throw finish.
- Target points (map in diagrams): head — Tai-Yang (temple), Chen-Zhen, Ren-Zhong (under nose), Lian-Quan and Tian-Tu (throat), Er-Men/Yi-Feng (by ear), Jia-Che (jaw), Jie-Chi; torso — Qi-Hai (lower abdomen), groin, Ru-Gen, Qi-Men, Ri-Yue (ribs), Jiu-Wei, Fu-Tu; leg — Zhong-Du, Liang-Qiu, Du-Bi, Zui-Xiai (knee/shin).
- Characteristic finishes: diagonal Kup Jarn elbow to head; foot strike to knee with knockdown; bending opponent's elbow over one's shoulder (pain-compliance lever risking dislocation — Technique 13); shoulder throw with elbow pressure (T.8-9); rear neck grip at back of head with pull + groin knee (T.17-22); circular leg sweep; palm push knocking opponent to ground (T.25).
- Against kicks: leave the line + double Guzn Sau block, catch kicking leg, hold + knee/straight kick to groin, topple (T.21, 23, 24, 26, 28).

## Philosophy and training (scene background)
- Yin-Yang: hard force neutralized by soft torso rotation, attack deflected “into emptiness” tangentially; soft flexible arm (Yin) strikes from beneath a hard block (Yang). Visually: fighter barely moves from the spot, everything decided by 45–90° turns and hand work.
- Training routine: paired Chi Sao (“sticky hands”), forms repeated 2–3 times, wooden dummy, wall bag for striking power, piercing a paper sheet with Biu Jee fingers, masters train twice a day for 6–8 hours.
- Third-year curriculum; dummy — final stage of the graded program (book 1 — first level, book 2 — second, book 3 — advanced).

## Scene sequences
1. **“Spear fingers”:** Pak Sau parries straight punch → Biu Jee thrust to throat (Tian-Tu) → step forward + diagonal Kup Jarn elbow to head → opponent crumples. (T.7-8)
2. **“Adding velocities”:** Lap Sau grabs punching arm → pull toward oneself + intercepting Yat Chi Kuen to chin → Jau Kuen from below to solar plexus → straight kick to knee. (T.2-3)
3. **“Throat chop”:** Tan Sau deflects punch → Jut Sau palm edge to throat (Lian-Quan) → neck grip at back of head, pull downward → groin knee. (T.6, 17)
4. **“Wing and elbow”:** Bong Sau parries cross upward-sideways → slide into Lap Sau → horizontal Gwai Jarn elbow to jaw → descending Kup Jarn finish. (T.4, 20)
5. **“Leg catch”:** move off the kick's line → double Guzn Sau block, shin grab → straight kick to groin above supporting leg → topple, finish from above. (T.21, 24)
6. **“Elbow lever”:** both hands catch punching arm (Jut Sau) → painful bend over shoulder with 180° turn, back to opponent → shoulder throw to ground. (T.8-9, 13)
7. **“Battering-ram palm”:** Fook Sau neutralizes combination → rising Wun Jeui palm heel to chin (head snaps back) → second Wun Jeui to floating ribs → side punch to temple (Tai-Yang). (T.1, 5)
8. **“Dummy staccato”:** training scene — arcing steps around Muk Yan, Pak Sau/Tan Sau/Bong Sau series on wooden “arms” with resonant knocks, Wun Jeui to “torso,” kick to “knee,” final double upward palm strike.
9. **“Solo Biu Jee Tao”:** form as combat meditation — frontal stance, fan of finger thrusts with 90°/180° angle changes, Kup Jarn elbow series on turns, Huen Sau rotations, deep bending wave and explosive straightening.
10. **“Phoenix Eye”:** Fook Sau + Biu Jee to eyes → Gum Sau presses both opponent's arms downward → straight “Phoenix Eye” fist with exposed phalanx to facial point → opponent blinded and falls. (T.13)
