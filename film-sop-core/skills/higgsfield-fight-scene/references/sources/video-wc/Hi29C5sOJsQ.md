# Wing Chun For Beginners Part 1: Wing Tsun Stance - Yee Ji Kim Yeung Ma (Hi29C5sOJsQ, 8:21)

Channel: Dan Lok (onscreen caption: “Trained by Bruce Lee's Original Students Ted Wong & Joe Lewis”). “Wing Chun For Beginners” series, part 1.

**Essence:** Breakdown of Wing Chun's basic training stance — Yee Ji Kim Yeung Ma (二字拑羊馬, “character ‘two’ goat/sheep-clamping stance”). Main point: this is NOT a fighting stance, but a “weighted vest” / foundation — a training platform from which one learns to explode. Demonstrates correct foot placement and common mistakes (leaning, excessive width, losing the center).

## Techniques by timestamp
- 0:02–0:24 — intro: the stance = the foundation of the entire Wing Chun system; “without a foundation you cannot build a skyscraper.”
- 0:36–0:56 — foot placement: start neutral, feet together → turn toes outward “as wide as possible” → second pivot on the balls of the feet, heels outward → toes point INWARD (pigeon-toed, adducted position).
- 0:56–1:50 — etymology: Yee Ji = the character “two” (二): two horizontal lines = the line of the feet + the line of the knees/hips above.
- 1:55–2:53 — Kim Yeung Ma: Ma = stance/“horse,” Kim Yeung = “clamping a sheep” with the thighs; all force goes INWARD (inward compression through knees/hips), a mnemonic for passing on knowledge before YouTube.
- 2:19–2:30 — stated explicitly: “this is not how you fight in Wing Chun; you cannot move in this stance” — it is a training tool.
- 3:20–3:43 — width: slightly wider than the shoulders; demonstrates “too wide = karate stance” (a deep, wide horse stance as a counterexample).
- 4:00–4:23 — errors in profile: excessive pelvic tilt/leaning backward = loss of balance; correct — neutral pelvis, “sit on it,” you can sit for hours; the form is practiced in this seated position.
- 4:30–5:06 — history: an underground art of Canton, the “red boats” (red boat opera) — cramped, rocking decks gave rise to a narrow, compact stance requiring no equipment.
- 5:06–5:52 — “weighted vest” concept: the stance = added weight; you learn to explode from it; “fighting like this is painting a wall”; the stance = a “dictionary” showing the idea and philosophy of the techniques.
- 6:00–6:44 — assembling the final stance: weight centered, focus inward, center of gravity neither high nor low (neutral), shoulders lowered, relaxed; forms and chain punches are performed from this base (“right, right, right” — a series of straights from the stance, ~6:44).
- 6:54–7:12 — self-check: feel the center of gravity and balance; you do NOT stand this way in combat — “your groin is open, your throat is open.”
- 7:46 — author's background: studied under four Wing Chun instructors, all from Yip Man lineages.
- 8:16 — ending: “Be water, my friend.”

## Visual details for Wing Chun choreography
- YJKYM stance: feet slightly wider than the shoulders, toes turned inward (~30–45°), knees bent and drawn inward (thighs clamping the “sheep”), pelvis tucked, “seated” posture, back vertical, shoulders lowered, hands held at the centerline during the form.
- Two-stage stance entry — a cinematic beat: pivot on the heels (toes outward) → pivot on the balls of the feet (heels outward) → settle; reads as the fighter “clicking” into a platform.
- Errors as contrast for a beginner character: torso tilted forward/backward, stance spreading into a wide karate horse, wandering center.
- Key for scenes: the hero does NOT fight from YJKYM; he “explodes” from it — transitioning to a fighting position with a step/turn. The stance suits training scenes (Siu Nim Tau form, wooden dummy, meditative stillness).
- Sticky contact/chain punches are only hinted at here: at ~6:44, a series of fast straight punches (chain straight punches) directly from the stationary stance — hands work along the centerline, torso motionless.
- Scene tempo: prolonged stillness + tiny pivot movements, then a sudden explosion — a useful contrasting rhythm for editing.
- Video location: a penthouse with floor-to-ceiling glass walls, harbor/marina view, wooden floor; instructor all in black — solo lesson without a partner.
