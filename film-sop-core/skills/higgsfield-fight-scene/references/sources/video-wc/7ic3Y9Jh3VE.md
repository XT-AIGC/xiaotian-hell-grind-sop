# Wing Chun Basic Techniques part 1 (7ic3Y9Jh3VE, 9:37)
Channel: V78thgz (reupload of a classic instructional film from the VHS era; demonstrated by “Master Wong” in a black kung fu uniform, with a partner in a white shirt, a bright studio background, and blue chapter title cards).

**Essence:** A canonical video tutorial on basic Wing Chun theory: centerline, economy of motion, fixed elbow, body turns (shifting), simultaneous block-strike, the “four gates,” trapping, chain straight punches, and three basic blocks (Tan Sau, Yuen Sau, Wu Sau). An ideal source of terminology and the style's logic for choreography.

**Techniques by timestamp**
- 0:02–0:28 — Introductory theory: a scientific approach, simplicity + economy of motion; the techniques are “structurally faster” than those of other styles.
- 0:28–1:25 — Centerline principle: an imaginary line through the middle of the body; its path is defined by crossing the wrists while extending the arms down and up (the opening movement of all three forms). Attack the center and the opponent must absorb the full impact; a strike to the side “slides off” during an evasive turn.
- 1:28–2:15 — The straight punch is the main weapon: the fist starts at the center (in front of the solar plexus), and both hands travel along the same path, perpendicular to the centerline. The footage includes anatomical diagrams of vulnerable points along the center.
- 2:16–2:40 — Fixed elbow principle: the lead elbow always stays a fist's distance from the centerline; the hand blocks, but the elbow remains a fixed pivot; an outward elbow = an opening.
- 2:42–3:40 — Shifting and angling: the body rotates around the centerline axis; defensively, it rolls away from punches that penetrate the guard; offensively, it creates new angles + increased reach; power comes from a sharp twist of the waist and hips.
- 3:41–4:15 — Simultaneous block-punch: the block and strike are one movement, not two; after striking, the retracting fist automatically blocks.
- 4:17–6:05 — Four gates: a shoulder-to-shoulder line + the centerline divide the area into four gates; Master Wong defends the upper gates against a series of punches with simultaneous block-strikes; the lower gates use alternating blocks with chopping strikes; the hands work in unison, with blocks coordinated with body turns.
- 6:12–6:35 — Trapping hands: whenever possible, grab, pin, and immobilize the opponent's arms, and attack on the same count.
- 6:36–8:20 — Hand attacks: a straight punch from the solar plexus to the opponent's nose level; one hand punches while the other retracts (the chain mechanism); a distinctive wrist snap lets one hand deflect and strike simultaneously; the one-inch punch delivers shocking power at short range; finger thrusts (bil jee) work like punches, with the fingers held rigid; palm thrust — the heel of the palm under the nose.
- 8:14–8:27 — Principle: force never clashes with force; a greater force is deflected by a lesser one.
- 8:27–9:05 — Tan Sau (palm-up block): a block with the thumb side of the forearm; at the end, the elbow returns to the fixed-elbow position (a fist's distance from the center); power comes from forearm rotation + a sharp palm-up turn + twisting the torso as the body turns.
- 9:05–9:37 — Yuen Sau (circling block): relaxed rotation of the hand around the wrist, training wrist flexibility and quick changes of hand position; the same hand that circles the attacking arm immediately attacks. (Final card: 3. Wu Sau / check block — cut off, continued in part 2.)

**Visual details for Wing Chun choreography**
- Stance: narrow and frontal, knees drawn inward, hands on the centerline (man-sau in front, wu-sau at the chest), elbows tucked — a “pyramid” in front of the sternum.
- Sticky contact: paired demonstrations take place at touching-forearm range; the hands do not lose contact with the partner's arms — the block flows into a trap and strike.
- Chain punches: alternating fist “pistons” along one tubular path, vertical fists, a wrist snap at the end; the series reads as an even machine-gun burst.
- Distance: everything is at close/medium range; the punch starts at the chest, not the shoulder; no windups or wide arcs.
- Tempo: even staccato without pauses — block-strike in one beat; body turns (shifts) are inserted between combinations as changes of angle, not retreats.
- Period look: static camera, bright background, master in black, student in white — useful as a clean silhouette reference.
