# Wing Chun For Beginners Part 2: Basic Wing Chun Block - Tan Sao (xsXAK3DtbDc, 11:32)
Channel: Dan Lok (Sifu Dan + partner Shin). Filmed in a room by panoramic harbor-view windows, both in black T-shirts, working face to face at medium-close range.

**Essence:** Detailed breakdown of tan sao (“dispersing hand”) — Wing Chun's basic “block” against a hook/sucker punch. Key idea: tan sao is NOT a passive block but a forward attacking structure along the centerline; all mechanics originate at the elbow, not the hand, with a completely relaxed shoulder. ASR writes “He-Man” in the transcript — this means Yip Man.

**Techniques by timestamp**
- 00:43–01:05 — context: tan sao against a hook / sucker punch (“one of the most common attacks”); partner demonstrates a swing from the side, Dan covers it.
- 01:14–02:10 — tan sao mechanics from stance: palm supinated (“holding a plate”), arm goes NOT sideways but INTO THE CENTER; attention focuses on the elbow, not the hand (“focus on the hand — amateur”); elbow drives inward toward the centerline; arm relaxed, “neither tense nor floppy.”
- 02:08–02:25 — frames: Dan slowly extends tan sao down the center, showing how the forearm covers the centerline.
- 02:25–03:20 — structural error: if tan sao moves sideways/outward (elbow spills out), the partner's push displaces the whole body; correct structure (elbow inward) means the push does not move the torso. Frames: Shin presses the forearm, Dan stands rooted.
- 03:05–03:15 — “correct tan sao is an attack, not defense”: arm thrusts forward, partner feels pressure into the chest.
- 03:28–04:00 — fingers: no “lazy,” loose fingers, hand like a plate, thumb TUCKED inward (otherwise it breaks if you miss); hand “like a spear.”
- 04:07–04:25 — angle: forearm ~45°, elbow a “fist under the ribs” away from the body; this is the structure's fixed distance.
- 04:35–05:10 — pressure test: tiny elbow/hand angle adjustment instantly changes stability (“this much already makes a difference”); frames: Shin presses, Dan adjusts the angle by centimeters.
- 05:13–05:40 — Yip Man practiced tan sao extremely slowly for an hour — like meditation (similar to Siu Lim Tao).
- 05:41–06:35 — relaxation down the chain: fingers first, then forearm, shoulder, hip, torso — “like bamboo: springy, not tense”; tense shoulder = lost structure even with the correct shape.
- 06:50–07:20 — coaching Shin: “relax your fingers, focus on the elbow, release the shoulder” — adjustment layer by layer.
- 07:26–08:40 — application against a straight punch: tan sao travels THROUGH the attacker's arm with simultaneous counterpressure; done “defensively” (simply held up), it gets penetrated. Timing: too late — you get hit, too early — no contact; view the opponent's forearm as a “bar” to CROSS and cut through.
- 08:42–08:55 — partner punches at full power (“as hard as you could”) — tan sao holds in relaxation, without flinching.
- 09:10–09:40 — two variations: classic palm-up tan sao and “inverted” (flip into a cover); Dan prefers the direct version — the hand is already loaded to continue striking the face (“directness”).
- 09:41–10:10 — paired drill: one throws straight punches, the other meets them with tan sao, switching sides; “experiment with the angle, feel it.”
- 10:20–11:10 — “toss a coin”: finishing slap/strike from tan sao without a windup — hand flicks like a coin, no pushing; “only the fingers — yet plenty of power”; frames: sharp slap from contact.

**Visual details for Wing Chun choreography**
- Stance: frontal, narrow, knees inward; partners almost chest to chest — elbow range, shorter than boxing range.
- Sticky contact: forearm to forearm, pressure tested with pushes — body does not move away; structure absorbs, not muscles.
- Mechanics read through the elbow: elbow slides INWARD and FORWARD toward the center; “plate” hand, thumb tucked.
- In combat, tan sao looks like “wedging” into an incoming punch + a simultaneous response with the other hand (block = attack, one beat, not two).
- Tempo: extremely slow (meditative) teaching, application — an explosive short impulse without a windup (coin-toss slap from contact).
- Incorrect version for onscreen contrast: elbow spilled outward, arm sideways, body swept away by a push — suitable for the “victim.”
