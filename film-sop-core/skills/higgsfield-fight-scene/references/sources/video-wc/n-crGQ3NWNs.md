# Wing Chun Basic Techniques part 2 (n-crGQ3NWNs, 9:36)
Channel: V78thgz (continuation of the same classic video tutorial; Master Wong, black kung fu uniform; paired drills by a green wall with calligraphy scrolls, form performed against a white background with giant calligraphy).

**Essence:** Second part of the basics: remaining blocks (Wu Sau, Fook Sau, Pak Sau, Jum Sau, Gan Sau, Bong Sau), low kicks, stance and steps (shuffle / circle stepping), wall-bag training, and the first form, Siu Lim Tau. A complete “dictionary” of Wing Chun defenses for fight choreography.

**Techniques by timestamp**
- 0:00–0:43 — Wu Sau (check block): block with the palm or its edge; the hand finishes in a “prayer” position before the chest; this is the rear backup hand, used when a punch penetrates the forward defense; blocks outward with the edge or inward with the palm.
- 0:50–1:20 — Fook Sau (bent-wrist block): block with the inside of the wrist; finishes in fixed elbow (a fist's distance from the centerline); a key element of sticking hands (Chi Sau).
- 1:21–1:40 — Pak Sau (cross-palm block): parry with the heel of the palm and a wrist snap; often combined with a punch (pak+punch in one beat).
- 1:41–2:10 — Jum Sau (inside drop block): diagonal downward chop with the edge of the palm/forearm; tiny range of motion — the elbow stays at the center; body rotation adds effectiveness.
- 2:11–2:40 — Gan Sau (downward slash block): downward forearm movement against groin/lower-level attacks and kicks; elbow hinge + shifting; usually against kicks.
- 2:41–3:05 — Bong Sau (wing block): an “unconventional” block — the only one that departs from fixed elbow; rolling wing: a rolling forearm motion (the forearm is the blocking surface), necessarily combined with torso rotation.
- 3:06–4:40 — Leg attacks: kicks are for “harassment” or closing distance, always supporting the hands; fast, low, direct — groin, knees, shins. Front heel kick — straight from the ground with a knee snap, never above the torso. Cross stomp kick — with the sole, breaks the opponent's balance for a hand finish. Low side kick — stomping strike with heel/sole. Combinations: cross stomp → side kick to the torso (when the opponent evades the stomp); front kick → sweep/trip (after a block, using its own force — follow the blocking force).
- 4:42–5:40 — Stances: basic stance — shoulder-width or slightly wider, knees slightly bent; “coiled” — a compressed spring, stability + mobility. Shuffle: forward — lead foot steps ~15 cm, rear foot follows; backward — the reverse; forward propulsion from the rear foot, backward from the front foot.
- 5:42–6:06 — Circle stepping: switching left/right stance; rear foot slides forward in an arc; used for cutting off angles and pressuring a retreating opponent.
- 6:06–6:48 — Wall-bag training: fist and palm strikes to a wall bag; “feeling penetration,” conditioning skin and bones; train slowly and progressively.
- 6:49–9:30 — Siu Lim Tau (“little idea form”): Wing Chun has only three forms, containing all the style's theory and techniques. The first contains almost all basic elements (stance, turns, blocks, punches, finger strikes) and principles (centerline, elbow, four gates). Performed slowly from a stationary stance — teaches hand coordination on both sides of the body. Method: theory first → individual elements → combinations → form section by section → entire uninterrupted run “in unison with the master.”
- 9:34 — title: Siu Lim Tau (Form Breakdown) — breakdown of the form (continues in the next part).

**Visual details for Wing Chun choreography**
- Wu Sau reads as a “shield palm” at the chin — the recognizable rear-hand silhouette in any Wing Chun stance; man sau (front) + wu sau (rear) is the basic guard.
- All blocks are short, the elbow barely moves; all the “range” comes from body rotation; on camera, it looks like tiny forearm snaps with a rotating torso.
- Bong Sau is the most cinematic: elbow rises, forearm rolls the attacking arm outward — the classic “bridge” into lap sau/trap.
- Legs: all kicks below the waist, from stance without a windup; a stomp to the knee/shin interrupts the rhythm between hand combinations; the kick is always linked to the hands (kick+punch on one beat).
- Steps: a short sliding shuffle (~15 cm) — the fighter “drives into” the opponent; circle step — an arcing stance change to cut off retreat.
- Siu Lim Tau against calligraphy: slow, hypnotic movements of one hand with a stationary torso — an excellent reference for a “meditative” training scene.
- Paired drills: attacker in white, master in black; distance — crossed forearms, contact unbroken.
