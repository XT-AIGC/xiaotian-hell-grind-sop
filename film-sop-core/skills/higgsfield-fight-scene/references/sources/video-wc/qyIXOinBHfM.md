# Wing Chun For Beginners Part 3: Basic Wing Chun Techniques - Bong Sao (qyIXOinBHfM, 10:48)
Channel: Dan Lok (Sifu Dan Lok + partner Shin)

**Essence:** Breakdown of bong sao (“wing arm”) mechanics — Wing Chun's most recognizable defense. Main point: bong sao is NOT an upward elbow flap like a “chicken wing,” but a fixed angle with the elbow staying in place and intention directed forward into the opponent. Presented as the “last line” — a reflex defense when the attack has already caught you late, not the first technique of choice.

**Techniques by timestamp**
- 0:52–1:09 — typical error: bong sao “from the elbow,” an upward chicken-wing flap (often after tan sao in films).
- 1:14–1:37 — correct mechanics: elbow barely moves, fingers “touch an imaginary wall” at a constant distance; forearm rotates around a stable elbow.
- 1:40–2:30 — partner pressure test: if the hand is too close/far or the elbow too high, pressure (push down / turn) collapses the structure and the person gives way.
- 2:32–2:50 — checklist: shoulders lowered, fingers pointing at the opponent's throat (in tan sao too) — “forward energy.”
- 2:53–3:24 — centerline: elbow does not cross the centerline (otherwise the side opens), but does not fall short of it either (chest opens). Angle ~45°, fingers toward the opponent.
- 3:24–4:03 — tan sao → bong sao transition, side view; correct bong has “spring power”: continuous forward pressure, not a rigid brace.
- 4:05–4:57 — criticism of “bong sao + lap sao” against a straight punch: looks good in films, but three movements against one punch is slow. Personally prefers pak sao / lap sao as the first defense.
- 5:00–6:19 — origin: bong sao = a “refined” natural flinch when something flies at the face; it is a last resort, defense for the losing fighter.
- 6:52–7:53 — key detail: intention in the ELBOW, elbow “thrusts” into the opponent — bong becomes aggressive, almost an attack, rather than a passive shield.
- 7:55–8:44 — against flare (flattening the elbow outward); correctness can only be checked by feeling through contact (chi sao with an instructor/partner).
- 8:46–9:43 — correcting the partner in live contact: “too much up,” “too much off to the side,” “too high”; finding gaps through pure touch — “he didn't tell me, it's all sensitivity.”
- 9:44–10:20 — method: tan sao → bong sao many times, slowly, in front of a mirror; “looks simple, not simple.”

**Visual details for Wing Chun choreography**
- Stance: both stand square, narrow, knees soft; all hand work at the upper level, feet almost static — a “conversation of hands.”
- Distance: constant elbow contact, partners a forearm apart; checks use PRESSURE, not punches — bodies sway under pressure when the structure is wrong (an excellent beat marker: “structure breaks → torso collapses”).
- Sticky contact: corrections without words — Sifu presses the arm, student adjusts the angle; opening = instant light slap to chest/throat.
- Geometry for the shot: elbow-pivot stationary, hand describes an arc; spear-fingers toward the throat; forearm angle 45°; shoulders always lowered.
- Tempo: demonstrations slow and drawn out (press/sliding), explosions short — one beat for “boom, opening.”
- Setting: bright loft studio, panoramic harbor windows (cruise ship in the background), both in black T-shirts — readable arm silhouettes against a bright background.
