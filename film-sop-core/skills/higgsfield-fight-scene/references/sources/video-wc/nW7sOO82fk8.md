# Wing Chun Basic Techniques part 3 Chinese Fight Art (nW7sOO82fk8, 7:13)
Channel: V78thgz (same classic series; Siu Lim Tau breakdown with paired combat applications + introduction to Chi Sau; bright studio, hand close-ups, title cards).

**Essence:** Decoding the first form's movements (Siu Lim Tau) into real applications: every gesture in the form = a block, circling action, pull, or strike. Then — Chi Sau (“sticky hands”): why forearm sensitivity is needed and how the Dan Sau (single sticky hands) cycle works. The core of Wing Chun's “sticky” vocabulary for choreography.

**Techniques by timestamp**
- 0:07–0:30 — Tan Sau in application: a sharp final forearm twist strengthens the block; always paired with a simultaneous punch (tan da).
- 0:31–0:52 — Huen Sau (circling palm): circles the opponent's arm after a block; one hand both blocks and attacks; a palm thrust follows naturally after circling.
- 0:53–1:10 — Pak Sau (palm slap): palm slap defending the upper gates; works best with a head slip / body evasion (slip + pak).
- 1:11–1:40 — Downward palm block: against lower-gate attacks, kicks, and knees; variation — breaking free from an elbow-lock attempt.
- 1:40–1:55 — Shoulder + forearm shrug: a shoulder-and-forearm “shrug” throws the attacker away (释 from the form).
- 1:56–2:15 — Double rear palm strike: double backward palm strike — primary defense against a rear bear hug; followed by a throw.
- 2:16–2:35 — Double knife-edge chop: double chopping strike with the palm edges; main target — throat.
- 2:36–3:12 — Palm jerk block: palm block-and-pull — the same hand blocks and jerks the opponent into a counterstrike (pulling into the strike).
- 3:12–3:45 — High wrist block → low palm block with one hand: demonstrates “a punch instantly becomes a block, a block becomes a punch” (the same limb, without a pause).
- 3:42–4:03 — Gan Sau (low block) in application: lower-gate defense + simultaneous knife-hand strike with the other hand.
- 4:04–4:30 — Bong Sau → double palm push: after the wing — double palm push, opponent flies back.
- 4:30–5:10 — Palm thrust to the throat after palm jerk — breaking the opponent's grip; alternating finger thrusts from the form = a second meaning: grip escape, immediately followed by a strike.
- 5:11–5:45 — Chi Sau “sticky hands”: a paired exercise for hand coordination (one blocks, the other strikes) and tremendous forearm sensitivity; teaches instinctive reading of the opponent's force direction and redirection with minimum movement.
- 5:45–7:12 — Dan Sau (single sticky hands): basic cycle. Partner A: Tan Sau → low palm thrust → Bong Sau. Partner B: Jum Sau → straight punch → Fook Sau. Partner B's Fook Sau “rests” on partner A's Tan Sau; tan converts to a low palm thrust, caught by jum; huen/tan changes to bong against B's straight punch. Both cycle in response to one another's techniques with a “firm yet relaxed flow of force.” Final title: Poon Sau (rolling sticky hands) — continued in the next part.

**Visual details for Wing Chun choreography**
- Chi Sau is the style's calling card: two fighters face to face, forearms stuck together, hands rolling in a wave (tan-bong-fook), torsos almost still — a “conversation with hands.”
- Distance throughout this part is point-blank: elbow to elbow; every form movement is shown solo first (slowly), then with a partner (sharply) — a good editing pattern of “form → application.”
- One hand = two actions: block flows into a circle (huen), pull (jerk), or strike without retracting — in choreography, this reads as a continuous hand spiral.
- Palm jerk: pull the opponent's arm toward yourself → intercepting palm strike — a striking “pull in and meet” beat.
- Rear-grab defense (double rear palm + throw) — a ready-made stage beat against a grappler.
- Paired tempo: attack on one count — answer on that same count; no “strike-pause-strike” exchange.

**Quotes**
- «A punch may be immediately transformed into a block, or vice versa — a block may be transformed into a punch.»
- «Chi Sau promotes tremendous sensitivity in the arms... to instinctively determine the direction of an opponent's force.»
- «Both partners perform these movements in response to each other's complementary techniques with a firm yet relaxed flow of force.»
- «The palm jerk pulls the opponent into the strike.»
