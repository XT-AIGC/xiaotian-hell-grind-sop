# Wing Chun For Beginners Part 5: Basic Wing Chun Techniques - Wu Sao (D5XHHW_ut0I, 7:21)

Channel: Dan Lok, “Wing Chun For Beginners” series, part 5. Paired lesson: Dan Lok + partner Shini, the entire lesson at arm's length.

**Essence:** Wu Sao (護手, “protective hand”) — a vertical open palm at the centerline between oneself and the opponent, at their face/throat level. Key: power comes from the ELBOW, not the fingers; fingers held tightly together, thumb tucked; the correct palm angle makes the structure “solid” without tension. Wu Sao is not only defense: changing the palm to a fist + extending the elbow instantly turns it into a strike.

## Techniques by timestamp
- 0:06–0:24 — intro: Wu Sao = protective hand, “something between you and the opponent” when caught by surprise.
- 0:44–1:03 — shape: the palm is positioned in front of the partner's face/throat; focus on the elbow, not the fingers — “imagine thrusting your elbow into him.”
- 1:08–1:26 — straight blast (chain straight punches) test: deliberately incorrect Wu Sao is “soft, everything gets through”; correct Wu Sao stands in front of the opponent, is “very difficult to penetrate,” and covers a large part of the torso.
- 1:30–1:59 — hand errors: “no lazy fingers” — do not splay the fingers, thumb in; an exposed finger gets broken/grabbed (demo: the partner grabs an exposed finger — “this is no good”).
- 2:00–2:32 — palm angle: tilted rather than “flat upward”; partner test: the same block at the correct angle feels “tougher,” its mechanics “a lot more solid”; “little difference, little mechanic.”
- 2:49–3:10 — “intention / stuff” (Chinese term, ≈ jin/neijin): not brute force, but a little “filling” — then Wu Sao “cuts through” the attack.
- 3:11–3:38 — attacking version: “this is not just defense”; changing the open palm to a fist + slightly extending the elbow = a strike; block→strike transition without resetting the position.
- 3:47–3:54 — entry: slow forward Wu Sao, from contact “I can come in right from here” — entering to strike from the line of contact.
- 3:56–4:50 — anti-straight-blast: the partner throws chain punches at real speed; one Wu Sao hand in front of his face closes the attack line — “not easy” to penetrate; tiny movements of one hand deflect the series.
- 4:51–5:03 — two hands: Wu Sao + the other hand = “now you're pretty safe” (excluding kicks).
- 5:05–5:31 — formula: “Center line, elbow, push”; side view: elbow not too far out (you will get hit), not too low (it will be pulled down), a “dense” position.
- 5:36–6:13 — structure vs force: “not force, it's just my mechanic, my angle is good”; feel a small “window” in the opponent's pressure and enter immediately: “just a tiny little bit — then you can go in.”
- 6:16–6:49 — outro: practice slowly, find an instructor; bridge to Bruce Lee / JKD.
- 7:18 — “Be water, my friend.”

## Visual details for Wing Chun choreography
- Stance: both fighters almost square to one another, feet shoulder-width apart, stationary — all the work is in the hands; distance — a bent arm, with forearms almost constantly in contact.
- Wu Sao arm: vertical forearm at the centerline, open palm tilted toward the opponent's face, fingers together and pointing up, thumb tucked, elbow lowered down and inward toward the center of the chest.
- Sticky contact: the Wu Sao palm/forearm rests on the partner's punching arm and “guides” it; straight blast combinations are neutralized through tiny shifts of one hand without retreating.
- Block→strike switch: frame to frame — the open palm at the opponent's face closes into a fist, the elbow extends to finish — reads as one beat without a windup (a signature Wing Chun moment for a hand close-up).
- Errors as beginner choreography: splayed fingers (the opponent grabs and bends a finger — a tiny joint-lock beat), a flat palm “collapses” under a strike.
- Tempo: slow positional explanations ↔ explosive 1–2-second bursts of chain punches; sound — sharp palm-on-forearm slaps (“slaps” in the transcript) — characteristic Wing Chun sound design.
- Location: the same penthouse with a glass wall and harbor/port view; Dan Lok in a black HTC T-shirt, partner in a dark T-shirt with a green print.
