# Wing Chun For Beginners Part 4: Basic Wing Chun Techniques - Guan Sao (xI1Uxxke7CY, 6:35)
Channel: Dan Lok (Sifu Dan + partner Shin). Same location: a room with panoramic harbor windows, both in black, working face to face at short range.

**Essence:** Guan sao — Wing Chun's low “block” against uppercuts, body punches, and low kicks. Key: neither a chopping karate block nor “catching” a strike, but DROPPING the arm downward from the elbow along the centerline (“dropping my hand toward my pocket”) + redirecting force rather than clashing with it. A punch immediately follows guan sao — the basic “guan sao → punch” drill.

**Techniques by timestamp**
- 00:00–00:06 — cold open: fast chain-punch combination (rapid volley of punches) in a paired drill.
- 00:29–00:45 — context: tan sao and bong sao cover the upper angles; guan sao is against uppercuts and low attacks; everything derives from the Siu Lim Tao form (“Little Idea”).
- 00:51–01:10 — how NOT to do it: “not a chopping motion,” not a sweeping karate block.
- 01:13–01:28 — mechanics: from its position, the arm simply GOES DOWN; movement comes not from the palm or wrist, but the ELBOW; image: “almost touching my pocket.” Frames: slow forearm drop down and inward along the body.
- 01:28–01:32 — works against both punches and kicks.
- 01:34–02:06 — error: a wide lateral block leaves a large opening and can miss; correct — “I'm not trying to catch the punch, I just DROP my hand” (drop, not catch), and it is easier to attack immediately from the drop. Frames: Shin punches the stomach, Dan briefly drops the punch downward.
- 02:06–02:20 — both sides: the hand is already at the center, the same drop mirrored; immediate counterpunch from guan sao.
- 02:30–03:00 — power test: partner punches harder and harder — “I'm not using force, I'm just putting an obstacle in the way” (body mechanic, structure, complete relaxation).
- 03:00–03:40 — against a kick: Shin throws a front kick, Dan “cuts” it downward with guan sao; slow demonstration: force travels inward — I do NOT strike against it (that causes injury), I REDIRECT it down and sideways. Frames (~185–195s): partner's raised leg, Dan's forearm sweeps the shin downward.
- 03:45–04:15 — range of motion: elbow lowered, movement is a “big sweep” covering a large sector at once — face above and stomach below; do not exaggerate by dropping the hand too low.
- 04:15–05:00 — a real fight is “all a blur”: you cannot tell fist from palm; high — cover the top (bong sao), low — guan sao; the technique forgives imperfect timing because it covers a large part of the body; “even if my timing is off, I'm still blocking something”; knives are a separate matter, this concerns empty hands only.
- 05:17–05:31 — drill: guan sao → punch, guan sao → punch (cyclically, alternating hands, one drops while the other punches).
- 05:31–05:55 — free flow punch drill: a free flow of punches and drops, “I'm doing it at 1/3 power, just flowing.” Frames: continuous rolling of hands in contact.
- 06:25–06:33 — ending: “Be water, my friend.”

**Visual details for Wing Chun choreography**
- Guan sao — short DOWNWARD forearm movement from the elbow along the body, hand finishing at hip/pocket; no wide windup.
- Against a kick, it looks like “cutting” the shin down and sideways with the forearm; torso stays upright, distance is maintained.
- Defense and strike — one beat: one hand drops down, the other simultaneously/immediately afterward punches straight to the face.
- Chain punches: a series of short vertical punches without pauses (opening frame 00:00), elbows down, fists rolling along the centerline.
- Drill tempo: continuous rolling “drop-punch-drop-punch,” relaxed, one-third power; in combat — one explosive drop + response.
- Contrast for the “victim”: a wide chopping block sideways, the body opens up, a miss.

**Quotes**
- «Not from my palm, not from my wrist — my elbow» — movement from the elbow.
- «I'm not trying to catch it, I just dropped it» — not catching the strike, dropping the arm.
- «Redirecting the force, not fighting the force» — redirect force, do not fight it.
- «Real fight is all a blur» — a real fight is a blur; the technique must cover a sector.
