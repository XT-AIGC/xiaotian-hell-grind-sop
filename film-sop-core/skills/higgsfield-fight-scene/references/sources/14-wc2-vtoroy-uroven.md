# 14 — Wing Chun: Second-Level Techniques

**Type:** primary-source book (scan, photo sequences) — Liu Weihan, “Wing Chun. Second-Level Techniques,” Sport-Press 2003, 140 pp., ~590 sequential illustrations.

**What it adds:**
- Complete phase-by-phase description of the second form, Chum Kiu Tao (“Bridge Across the Gorge”) — 180°/90°/45° turns, paired hands, kicks within the form.
- Expanded hand vocabulary: Yat Chi Kuen, Wun Jeung, Jau Kuen, Jut Sau, Gwai Jarn + Tan/Bong/Gum/Biu/Gan/Lap/Pak/Jum/Fook/Wu/Huen Sau defenses.
- 13 Chi Sau exercises (Koi Sau based on Poon Sau) and nine Lap Sau exercises — ready paired choreography of “contact → interception → strike.”
- 20 counterattacking techniques (defense+strike on one beat, joint-lock levers, head twists) and two-/three-strike attacking combinations with level changes.
- Map of vulnerable centerline points (Tan-tu, Jiu-wei, Qi-hai, Ju-xu, Zhong-du, etc.) — strike targets for prompts.

---

## 1. Principles (for scene physics)
1. Do not oppose force with force: deflect the attack, leave the line through a step/body turn.
2. Centerline: protect your own, strike points on the opponent's line.
3. Both hands simultaneously: one blocks, the other counters on the same beat (zero pause).
4. Whole-body power: foot → hip → lower back → shoulder → triceps → hand (a wave with body rotation).
- Close-range combat, primarily hand work; kicks no higher than the stomach (shin, knee, groin) + sweeps/trips.

## 2. Footwork (second level)
- **Forward/backward step-and-slide** — quickly close/open distance while switching stance (left↔right): weight onto lead leg, rear foot follows, body turns, hands exchange positions.
- **Forward zigzag step** — approach from the side at an acute angle to the attack line; first step = leave the line, second step = enter with a counterattack. Two variations: with and without switching stance.

## 3. Hand strikes (Cantonese terms)
- **Yat Chi Kuen** — straight vertical-fist punch (bases of little/ring/middle fingers); at level 2 — to vulnerable points, with a variation exposing a finger's second phalanx for pinpoint targets.
- **Wun Jeung** — palm-heel strike: vertical (to chin) or horizontal (to torso/internal organs); also works as a push.
- **Jau Kuen** — straight rising punch from below upward and forward at 30–45° (to chin, solar plexus, liver).
- **Jut Sau** — chopping strike with the palm edge (outer little-finger edge), sweeping elbow extension, target — throat/neck.
- **Gwai Jarn** — horizontal forward elbow strike; most powerful, almost impossible to block, delivered point-blank with body mass through torso rotation.
- **Finger thrust (biu-jee type)** — to eyes or Tan-tu point (below Adam's apple); fastest, often opens the attack.

## 4. Defenses (“sau” blocks)
- **Tan Sau** — deflecting block with the inside of the forearm, palm upward; elbow on the centerline, body turns further in the direction of movement.
- **Bong Sau** — “wing”: deflecting block with rotating forearm, opponent's arm knocked from outside inward; torso turn essential. Key movement of the entire Chum Kiu form.
- **Gum Sau** — downward pressing block with the palm heel (against middle/lower-zone strikes).
- **Biu Sau** — deflecting block with the outer forearm edge from inside outward (against straight and side attacks to the head), can act as a stopping block.
- **Gan Sau** — hard deflecting/stopping block with the outer forearm edge from inside outward (middle/lower zone, including kicks).
- **Lap Sau** — finger grip: immobilizing a limb, pulling down/toward oneself, entry to throws and joint locks.
- **Pak Sau** — slapping palm block; **Jum Sau** — pressing the opponent's forearm from above; **Fook Sau** — controlling “bridge” hand; **Wu Sau** — standby palm at the chest on the centerline; **Huen Sau** — circular wrist rotation (rolling around the opponent's arm).

## 5. Kicks
- **Straight kick** — heel (pushing, foot horizontal forward) or instep (shin whips upward from below); targets: shin, knee, stomach, groin. Phases: closing step → weight onto supporting leg → knee up → extension → exhalation. Supporting leg slightly bent, foot fully grounded, kicking leg tense at the finish. Delivered with either lead or rear leg (rear leg — with hand change: same-side hand returns to chest, opposite hand extends).
- **Side kick** — supporting foot pivots, body turns, leg extends sideways; high (stomach) — whole sole, low (shin/knee) — outer foot edge near heel. Used as a stop kick that disrupts the attack.
- **Straight stop kick** — foot turned toes sideways, strike to shin/knee of the attacking leg.
- Training equipment: wall bag, three posts in a triangle (straight+side kick combinations to posts with turns — a ready “one against three” scene).

## 6. Chum Kiu Tao form (what it looks like)
Second form, “Bridge Across the Gorge”: establish contact with the opponent's arms (“build a bridge”) → find an unprotected zone → lightning attack. Unlike Siu Lim Tao, performed WITH FOOTWORK and turns. Standard duration — 2–2.5 minutes, with brief muscular tension at the end of every movement.
Sequence skeleton (photo sequence, figs.89–239):
1. Opening: stance, fists to chest, spread feet into frontal stance; both arms down-forward (left over right), close fists, to chest.
2. Stationary series: left Yat Chi Kuen → open palm upward → Huen Sau (wrist rotation) → fist to chest; same on right.
3. Two-hand block: fingers forward → both arms forward-upward → turn to fighting stance, hands down before chest (palms down, left over right).
4. **180° turns with hands**: repeated left/right turns without changing hand positions, then alternating Pak Sau + Tan Sau (right/left), alternating Wun Jeung palm strikes.
5. **Turns with Bong Sau**: 180° turn → Bong Sau with one arm → bent arm horizontal before chest → Tan Sau with both → another turn and Bong Sau with the other arm (repeated cycles — signature section of the form).
6. Yat Chi Kuen forward → 90° turn to front → Jut Sau palm edge at shoulder level → finger thrust → Huen Sau → fist to chest.
7. **Kicks within the form**: Lap Sau toward shoulder → side kick with exhalation → step forward → Bong Sau → double Tan Sau → step-and-slide + Bong Sau (a “step-bridge” series) → rising Jau Kuen.
8. Straight kick (left/right), with Bong Sau in both arms after landing, double Tan Sau, low Bong Sau, Jum Sau (fingers upward), Wun Jeung with both.
9. Ending: 45° turns with Gum Sau left/right, Yat Chi Kuen series with frontal turns, Huen Sau, hands down, feet together.

## 7. Second-stage Chi Sau (Shun Chi Sau / Koi Sau)
Foundation — Poon Sau (“rolling hands”): partners in forearm contact, Tan/Bong/Fook Sau positions roll in circles. Exercises = explosion from rolling:
- **No.1:** palm strike to chest → partner's Jum Sau → their Yat Chi Kuen to head → your Tan Sau → their Fook Sau control → change through Poon Sau → Jum Sau + instant Yat Chi Kuen to chest.
- **Nos.2–3:** on feeling excessive pressure — Huen Sau (counterclockwise/clockwise wrist rotation) and palm-heel strike to chest.
- **No.4:** 45° body turn + circular release of contact → palm presses forearm → Yat Chi Kuen to head → second strike with other hand + Jum Sau (control of both arms).
- **Nos.5–7:** wrist pressure during Bong Sau → cross the opponent's arms (Jum Sau downward) → head strike; palm presses shoulder/arms downward → strike series, opponent's arms “tied up.”
- **Nos.8–9:** Jum Sau onto arm → Wun Jeung to head → Gan Sau control → Yat Chi Kuen with 45° turn; Pak Sau → Gan Sau covering both arms → head strikes.
- **Nos.10–13:** catches from beneath the elbow, Biu Sau against a wrist grab, grab + Jut Sau to neck → Jum Sau press → Wun Jeung to chin.
Final meaning: hands “stick” to the opponent; when they press, you roll; when they withdraw, your hand follows and hits the open zone; fighting with closed eyes is possible. Culmination — Koi Sau: a free combination “bout” of flowing Jum→Tan→Fook→Bong→Lap→Wu Sau with strikes (figs.313–352) — a ready reference for a long chi-sao scene.

## 8. Lap Sau technique (paired exercises)
Developing the ability to open the central zone: neutralize with one hand, simultaneously counterattack with the other. Typical cycle (exercise 1): Yat Chi Kuen to head → Bong Sau+Wu Sau → opponent pulls the arm with Lap Sau and strikes with a lateral fist → Pak Sau + arm rise → grab their arm → Yat Chi Kuen to head → their Pak Sau → Gan Sau + 45° turn → Gum Sau press → head strike. Further variations: Gum Sau sweep from the centerline → strike; Lap Sau grab + downward pull + liver/head strike; Lap Sau + straight kick to torso → Pak Sau→Gum Sau control → Wun Jeung to chin.

## 9. Attack technique (two- and three-strike combinations)
Principles: attack first; two-/three-strike combination — first deceptive/fast, rely on second/third; change levels (head→groin/knee is harder to defend); strikes without pauses.
Two-strike: I. step + eye thrust → left Yat Chi Kuen to head/throat. II. eye thrust (or Yat Chi Kuen) → straight left punch to solar plexus (Jiu-Wei). III. step + eye thrust → Gwai Jarn elbow to side of head. IV. eye thrust → right straight kick to groin/torso or side kick to knee. V. Yat Chi Kuen to head → low straight kick to supporting knee (“unnoticed”). VI–VIII. closing step → groin kick (Qi-Hai) → Yat Chi Kuen to head / eye thrust; groin kick → kick with inside of foot to knee → to shin (Ju-Xu).
Three-strike: eye thrust → Yat Chi Kuen to throat → Jau Kuen to solar plexus/chin; two thrusts/punches to head → groin or knee kick; eye thrust → solar-plexus punch → rising punch to chin; groin kick → head punch → eye thrust; groin kick → head strike → rising strike to solar plexus (or finish with stop kick to knee).

## 10. Defense and counterattack (20 techniques)
Three counterattack levels: 1) block, then strike; 2) block+strike simultaneously (Wing Chun's core); 3) preemptive strike at the attack's inception (master level).
Key scenarios (book numbering):
- **No.1:** step right off line + Tan Sau → two-hand arm grab (Lap Sau) → pull toward yourself + straight groin kick (Qi-Hai) → Yat Chi Kuen series to head (Jia-Che, Tai-Yang points).
- **No.2:** move left + Pak Sau → wrist grab + pull with turn → Jut Sau edge to throat → arm withdrawal → Yat Chi Kuen to solar plexus (Jiu-Wei).
- **No.3:** step left + right Tan Sau → simultaneous left Yat Chi Kuen to head → inside-foot kick to outside of knee.
- **No.4:** step left + Tan Sau → Yat Chi Kuen to liver → wrist grab + pull down-right → descending side kick to back of knee — opponent knocked onto their back.
- **No.5:** Jum Sau + grab → pull + kick to inside of knee → rising fist to torso (Qi-Hai) → rising strike to throat/chin.
- **No.6:** Biu Sau → Yat Chi Kuen to solar plexus → Gwai Jarn elbow to head (Tai-Yang/Er-Men) → reverse elbow to neck. Variation: Lap Sau at wrist and shoulder (elbow immobilization).
- **No.7:** two-hand grab → pull + liver kick → step → Gwai Jarn to throat → arm press, elbow to Fu-Tu point.
- **No.8:** Pak Sau → rising fist to liver/ribs → arm press → Gwai Jarn to head/throat.
- **No.9:** Tan Sau + Wun Jeung to ribs → wrist grab + rotation → elbow pressure (Qu-Chi point) → joint-lock lever, tipping face-first to the ground.
- **No.10:** Fook Sau → Wun Jeung to elbow joint → Huen Sau + turn → wrap the arm → elbow lever lock.
- **No.11:** Bong Sau + step left → Lap Sau + arm rotation → step between opponent's legs → their arm over your shoulder → sharp downward pull — elbow lever over the shoulder.
- **No.12:** Pak Sau → Wun Jeung to base of nose (Ren-Zhong) → step behind leg → head grab (chin+back of head) → neck twist (only as a “last resort” — cinematic finisher).
- **No.13 (against a kick):** retreat → Gan Sau → instep counterkick to groin → side kick with foot edge to knee.
- **Nos.14–15 (against a side kick):** step left-forward + Gan Sau → rising groin kick / side kick to back of supporting knee; or turn + leg catch (elbow bent, forearm upward) → descending elbow to thigh (Zhong-Du) → kick to back of supporting knee.
- **Nos.16–17:** two-strike series — alternating Tan/Pak/Biu Sau → extending Biu Sau → palm down onto shoulder → Yat Chi Kuen to ribs; Biu Sau → torso kick.
- **No.18 (against a round kick):** step TOWARD opponent (smothering the swing) + Pak Sau with turn → left Pak Sau + Wun Jeung to solar plexus / phalanx fist to Jiu-Wei.
- **No.19 (against a rising kick):** closing step + side stop kick to shin → Tan Sau + counterpunch to head/throat → shift left away from right hand.
- **No.20 (against a low kick):** raise left leg, knee blocks → Bong Sau → Lap Sau grab + downward pull → Yat Chi Kuen to chin.

## 11. Vulnerable points (targets)
Head: Tan-Yan (temple), Er-Men (by ear), Yi-Feng (behind jaw), Jia-Che (jaw angle), Chen-Zhen (bridge of nose/forehead), Ren-Zhong (under nose), Lian-Quan (under chin), Fu-Tu (side of neck), Tan-tu (below Adam's apple). Torso: Jiu-Wei (solar plexus), Qi-Hai (lower abdomen), groin. Legs: Zhong-Du (inner thigh), Ju-Xu (shin), knee from all sides. Arm: Qu-Chi (elbow crease — for levers).

## Scene sequences (ready beats)
1. **“Zigzag entry”:** zigzag step off line → second step + Tan Sau and Yat Chi Kuen on one beat → low stop kick to knee.
2. **“Bridge and break”:** forearm contact (bridge) → two Poon Sau rolls → Huen Sau release → Wun Jeung to chest → opponent flies back.
3. **“Pull down and finish”:** their jab → Bong Sau+Wu Sau → Lap Sau pulling arm downward → Jut Sau edge to throat → Yat Chi Kuen to solar plexus.
4. **“Double level”:** finger thrust to eyes (feint) → Yat Chi Kuen to throat → straight heel kick to groin → step → Gwai Jarn elbow to temple.
5. **“Anti-kick”:** their low kick → knee up (shin block) → foot down + Bong Sau against punch → Lap Sau pull → rising Jau Kuen to chin.
6. **“Lever finish”:** Tan Sau + Wun Jeung to ribs → wrist grab, arm rotation → elbow pressure (Qu-Chi) → face to floor.
7. **“Chi-sao duel”:** long take — sticky hands: Tan↔Fook↔Bong rolls, bursts of Jum Sau+strike, both neutralize and reenter; finish — Gum Sau pinning both arms and Yat Chi Kuen to head.
8. **“Three posts”:** training scene — straight kick to front post → without lowering leg, side kick to right post → weight transfer → left side kick to left post (surrounded by three).
9. **“Neck twist” (villain's finisher):** Pak Sau → Wun Jeung under nose → step behind leg → chin+back-of-head grip → sharp twist with body rotation.
10. **“Form onscreen”:** hero performs Chum Kiu alone: slow 180° turns with Bong Sau, acceleration, Lap Sau + side kick with sharp exhalation — camera circles.

**Mechanics for prompts:** every block = body turn (not pulling away); block and strike on the same frame-beat; short strikes without windups, from chest along centerline; vertical fist; kicks below the waist, supporting leg bent; after a grab — pull the opponent TOWARD yourself into the strike; final accent — brief muscular tension and exhalation.
