# 07 — Fighting Method (abridged edition)
**Type:** primary-source book (scan, 55 pp., Russian translation of “Bruce Lee's Fighting Method: Advanced Techniques,” Bruce Lee and M. Uyehara, Ohara Publications; photos from 1966)
**What it adds to choreography:** step-by-step photographic sequences “feint → opponent's reaction → real strike,” precisely identifying limb, technique, and target—ready mini-beats for fight scenes. Also JKD tactical logic: stop-hits, intercepting attacks during windup, opposite-stance work, fighter psychological types (mechanical/intellectual, nervous/calm, tall/short)—material for character and scene rhythm.

## Edition structure
Chapters XII–XVI of the full course (“Advanced Techniques” volume): hand attacks (I and II), kicking attacks, defense and counterattack, attributes and tactics. Sparring photos use dark uniforms, opponent in headgear—Lee unprotected (itself a frame-style decision).

## Hand attacks, part I (ch. XII)
- Principles: attack simple and direct; almost all JKD techniques indirect—after a feint or as counterattack. Attack when opponent's hand movement “opens a line.” Feint must disrupt opponent's plan and bring him within reach.
- **Finger jab to eyes**—“foundation of offense and defense”: lengthens reach by 7.5–10 cm and takes shortest path. Finish extension directly in front of your nose—do not expose upper zone.
- Sequence: downward feint with slight squat (simulate body attack) → opponent lowers guard hand → instant finger jab to eyes; place your right foot beside opponent's foot—blocks his counterkick.
- Against left lead: deceptive right-hand movement knocks away opponent's lead hand (removing the “obstacle” in the path) → quick eye strike; low shin kick also works as feint.
- **Right lead straight**—Jeet Kune Do's “bread”: sharp, precise, fast; power from hip rotation “in a fraction of a second”; vertical fist; punch finishes in front of your nose, rear hand protects head; committing shoulder adds ~10 cm reach.
- Synchronization/timing: impose rhythm on opponent, then break it; “change of synchronization”—slowing just before strike, a pause in the lunge opens defense. Attack when opponent prepares an attack, moves, changes fighting line—he cannot redirect until finishing the action begun.
- Economy of motion: tension is a brake; a free, unforced athlete is faster.
- **Lead body punch:** torso drops to target level, punch slightly upward/almost horizontal; target—solar plexus; left hand simultaneously blocks opponent's high strike.
- Combinations (ch. XII/XIII): head→body; straight→hook; right hook→left straight; three-strike series; “safety triple”—1st and 3rd strikes to same zone (body–jaw–body).

## Hand attacks, part II (ch. XIII)
- **Left straight to body:** delivered bent forward—safe; best counter against a facing right-lead opponent; weight on front leg, lead shoulder raised against counterhook.
- Sequence: deep false attack → opponent raises hands → duck with penetrating left to center of body, head against left shoulder (counter protection).
- **Backfist:** fast, neat, elusive; vertical semicircle directly from behind front hand, bottom to top; best target—temple. Trapping sequence: right hand restrains opponent's elbow + your front foot on his foot (suppresses kick) → switch hands: left immobilizes his arms, right delivers stepping backfist.
- **Short lead punch (jab):** not powerful but disrupts balance; series of short punches keeps opponent in a shell “without respite”; each following punch covers the previous one.
- **Hook:** not wide—light, energetic; arm driven by torso turn, follows body “like an arrow from a bow”; tighter elbow angle means sharper strike; rear hand shields face, rear elbow shields side. Weight transfers to rear leg, lead heel rises, hips counterclockwise. Sequences: rear straight feint → opponent drops lead hand to block → high hook to jaw; close-range hook to body/groin—groin harder to block than jaw.
- **Uppercut:** close combat against lowered head and “random swingers”; bend knees and straighten with strike, on balls of feet; lead hand withdraws to protect head.

## Kicking attacks (ch. XIV)
- Verse epigraph “Powerful Legs”: shin kick stops attack; side kick “breaks the back”; spinning kick is a surprise throwing opponent to ground; sweeping kick looks spectacular in cinema but has little practical power (direct statement of meaning—useful for “cinematic effect vs realism”).
- Kick before opponent establishes defense; coordinate knee and hip; kick high, low, at ground level, while moving and turning.
- **Lead shin/knee kick:** first strike of first encounter; can break knee with one hit. Sequence: wide upward lead-hand wave (distraction) → forward lunge → low knee kick; Lee remains beyond reach afterward.
- **Lead side kick**—JKD's most powerful strike: “even blocking cannot save you”; stronger from distance—momentum has time to build. Upward hand wave → side kick to knee; look at opponent's face, not target—conceal intent. Rib variant: throws opponent backward (photo sequence with flight—hit-reaction benchmark).
- If opponent does not react to feint—change tactic: high side kick over defensive hands straight to face.
- **Round kick**—most frequently used: fast, mobile, to head/body/groin. Sequences: feinted knee-kick attempt → opponent lowers hand → high round kick to face (including leaning torso, overhead view—striking angle); raised hand opens center → round kick to left side; false low round kick → opponent lowers left hand → high round kick to head.
- Feint rule: no more than two; first closes half the distance; feint lasts just long enough to provoke response, not allow a block.
- **Spinning kick:** use cautiously—back can be caught; usually a stop-hit. Upward hand wave → step to opponent's right → sharp turn on right leg without losing sight → spinning kick to midbody or face, throwing him backward; in JKD this is a frontal thrust toward target, not a wide sideways “slap.” One of few left (rear) leg strikes from guard; knocks opponent down through defense.
- **Sweeping kick (reverse/heel hook):** rare—opponent's hand always covers face, travels high, weak for knockdown; effective against careless opponent: disguised as front kick, bypasses low block, semicircular path left to right (book includes “leg path” diagram).

## Defense and counterattack (ch. XV)
- Philosophy: “deflecting strikes may look like defense, but it is the highest form of attack”; counterattack almost safe—opponent catches himself as he approaches. Better to provoke an attack with feint than wait.
- **Finger stop-hit:** intercept attack before it develops—opponent struck in eye before he can hit; demoralizes aggressor.
- Stop-hit sequences: against swing—step toward + lead straight to face (straight shorter than swing); against straight—intercept with right eye jab during windup.
- **Shin/knee stop-kick:** stops any hand or leg attack; against rear-leg forward step—meet striking leg halfway with kick; against low stance—evade fist and meet shin with kick; against left lead—slip + simultaneous lead-knee kick (“leg reaches farther than arm—a completely safe answer”).
- **Stop-side-kick:** strongest at middle/long range—one hit sufficient; against onrushing opponent—devastating chest side kick stops and throws him to floor; against rear-leg kick—answer with sliding kick during his supporting phase.
- After evasion, hit while opponent is “tense from lunging” or recovering—his leg is stationary then.
- Grappling sequence: false right lunge to face → opponent commits to right straight → one-motion parry and wrist grab → round kick to groin.
- Meeting round kick to face against crushing attack: pivot on rear leg while evading attack direction, firm balance.

## Attributes and tactics (ch. XVI)
- Speed types: perception, thought, execution, alteration (changing direction halfway). Emotion, fatigue, lack of training, and poor focus lengthen reaction.
- Mindset: winner confident and relaxed; obsession tightens muscles; great athlete conserves energy, movements neat and light.
- Scene character types: “mechanical” fighter—repetitive automatic strikes; “intellectual”—strategy, analysis, adaptation to enemy. Against nervous opponent—short feints and calm; against calm persistent opponent—feint to draw stop-hit, then trap; short fighter rushes close at protruding targets, tall fighter slow and keeps distance—do not retreat from him, enter his attack.
- Tactical devices: renewed attack as opponent withdraws arm/leg; series of high feints opens low targets (knee, shin); interrupted attack—pause before strike collapses defense; novice's chaotic rhythm itself deceives master—maintain range and intercept; oppose opponent's tactics (against counterpuncher—feints, against feinter—stop-hits; “fight a boxer as a wrestler, a wrestler as a boxer”).
- Ending: specialist “brings the whole arsenal into play, gaining opening after opening until delivering a powerful crushing blow”—a fight-scene climax formula.

## Overlap with the four-volume “Bruce Lee's Fighting Method”
**Almost completely duplicates it.** This Russian edition = volume 4, “Advanced Techniques,” of the same Bruce Lee's Fighting Method series (introduction explicitly says chapters XII–XVI, continuation of “Self-Defense Techniques,” “Basic Training,” “Skill in Techniques”). If the four-volume series has already been annotated, techniques and photos are identical.
Unique to this scan/translation:
- Russian terminology of the 1990s translation (“leg hook,” “rear fist strike,” “safety triple,” “short lead”)—useful for aligning the knowledge-base vocabulary.
- Compressed presentation: advanced layer only (attack, counterattack, tactics), without basic conditioning—convenient as a quick sequence reference.
- Uyehara's introduction with 1966 context and Linda Lee's position; verse chapter epigraphs (including spectacle vs practicality of kicks “in cinema”)—direct propositions about techniques' cinematic qualities.
- Distinctive photo-scene detail: opponent always in headgear, Lee unprotected; neutral background, 3–4-phase sequences—a ready storyboard template “feint → reaction → hit → flight.”
