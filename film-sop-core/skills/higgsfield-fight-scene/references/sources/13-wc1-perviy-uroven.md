# 13 — Wing Chun: First-Level Techniques (Liu Weihan, 2002)

**Type:** primary-source book (basic techniques of Yip Man lineage Wing Chun, first year of training)

**What it adds:**
- Exact geometry of Yee Ji Kim Yeung Ma and the forward fighting stance — for a correct WING CHUN silhouette onscreen.
- Complete vocabulary of basic hands (tan/bong/fook/pak/gan/jum/biu/huen/gum/jut/wu sau), with the mechanics and working zone of each.
- Canonical dramatic patterns: grab-pull + simultaneous strike (lap sau), “block+strike in one phase” sequences, chi sau rolling.
- Visual stages of Siu Lim Tao — ready material for training scenes (including on one leg).
- Rules of zonal defense (four body zones) and leaving the line — the logic of which hand covers what.

---

## Stance and structure

**Yee Ji Kim Yeung Ma (frontal training stance):**
- Weight 50/50 on both feet. Knees slightly bent and drawn INWARD (“clamping the goat”).
- Feet form a triangle: toes inward, heels apart; 35–40 cm between the midpoints of the feet.
- Pelvis slightly forward, thighs slightly tense. Torso upright, head aligned with the body.
- In the training version, fists at the armpits, elbows bent. Stance entry: feet together → toes out 30° → spread the heels while bending the knees.
- Visually: narrow, collected, “clamped” stance — the complete opposite of wide kung fu stances.

**Fighting hand position (man sau / wu sau):**
- Lead arm extended forward along the centerline, hand open, fingers forward and upward; elbow 15 cm from the body.
- Rear hand (wu sau) — open palm, fingers upward, closer to the chest, also ON the centerline.
- Both hands on one vertical line in front of the body's center. A right-handed fighter usually has the left hand in front (strong hand behind).

**Forward fighting stance:**
- Weight shifted to the REAR leg; lead leg almost unweighted (for quick steps and stop kicks).
- Feet almost parallel, knees bent and drawn toward each other (groin protection). Body turned 30–45°.
- Torso straight, head slightly lowered, gaze forward. Entry: from frontal stance — transfer weight to rear leg, bring lead leg forward in an arc.
- Errors for an “incorrect” fighter onscreen: rocking torso, lifting heels, losing the distance between the knees.

**Centerline (the key to everything):**
- Imaginary vertical dividing the body in half. Protect your own; attack points on the opponent's centerline.
- All punches and elbows move ALONG this line; the fist never leaves it from start to target.

## Footwork

- **Stationary turns (yun ma):** from frontal stance, turn the body 45° or 90°; weight shifts to one leg, feet pivot while remaining parallel; distance between knees unchanged. Turn = leaving the attack line + impulse for the strike (not retreat!). Body rotation lets the attack pass “into emptiness,” deflecting it tangentially.
- **Step-and-slide (foundation):** sliding steps from heel to toe, close to the floor. Forward: lead foot slides → rear foot follows. Backward/sideways: the foot closest to the direction moves first, the other follows. Stance unchanged.
- Torso does NOT rock, rise, or fall — head travels at one height (an important visual marker of the style).
- Steps short and equal in length; hand position stays unchanged while moving.
- Strike-and-step timing: single punch — as the rear foot follows; two-strike combination — first punch with the lead-foot step, second with the rear-foot follow-up.

## Hands (defensive positions with mechanics)

- **Pak Sau** (slapping palm): open-palm parry from outside inward, hand travels horizontally from the body's center toward the opposite shoulder (no farther). Against straight attacks to head/chest.
- **Tan Sau** (dispersing hand): deflecting block with the inside edge of the forearm from inside outward; forearm rotates along its axis, palm opens UPWARD, elbow on the centerline 15 cm from the body. Against straight attacks to the head/upper torso.
- **Bong Sau** (wing arm): parry with the outside of the forearm; arm rises while rotating along its axis — elbow above the hand, distinctive “bent” wing silhouette. Elbow firmly linked to shoulder, do not raise the shoulder, wrist on the centerline. Against straight attacks to head/torso — Wing Chun's most recognizable gesture.
- **Fook Sau** (covering/bridge hand): block with the inside of the forearm from outside inward, followed by CONTROL of the opponent's arm through the bent wrist (the “hook” hand rests on top). Elbow springs forward/backward in the centerline plane; pressure always forward, not downward.
- **Gan Sau** (downward chopping): parry/stop with the outer forearm edge from inside outward and down, palm turns downward. Against attacks to the middle and lower torso.
- **Jum Sau** (sinking): downward parry with the outer forearm edge to solar-plexus level. Against straight body punches + immobilizing the opponent's arms.
- **Biu Sau** (shooting fingers): elbow extends, forearm rotates palm-down, parry from inside outward. Against straight head punches; simultaneously = finger thrust to the eyes (block-strike in one movement).
- **Huen Sau** (circling hand): small wrist circle from inside outward — circling around contact, escaping a wrist grab; connecting movement between positions.
- **Wu Sau** (protective hand): vertical palm at the chest on the centerline — backup, ready to grab; with both hands — blocking circular strikes (including kicks to the head).
- **Gum Sau** (pressing palm): downward palm pressure (in the form — sideways, backward, forward).
- **Jut Sau** (jerking): short downward forearm jerk to solar-plexus level.
- **Lap Sau** (grabbing): grab the opponent's wrist + pull downward and toward yourself, taking them off balance.
- **Pair rule:** while one hand works high, the other covers low (and vice versa); free hand always near the body on the centerline.
- Arm condition: outwardly soft, “filled” within; stiffness only for a fraction of a second in the final phase of a block or strike.

## Strikes

- **Yat Chi Kuen (straight vertical fist)** — the style's main punch. Fist starts at the center of the chest and travels strictly along the centerline to chin level; contact through the bases of the lower three knuckles (little/ring/middle fingers). Arm relaxed throughout, explosive tension only in the final fraction of a second, elbow fully extends. Chain punches: right-left without pause, one replaces the other (one hand retracting = the other shooting out).
- **Yan Jeung (forward palm-heel strike)** and **Wun Jeung (forward-upward palm-heel strike)**, also **Wu Jeung** in the form — palm strikes “into” the target, for affecting internal organs; often immediately follow a block with the same hand.
- **Finger thrust (biu)** — to eyes or throat; fastest weapon, often used as an interception.
- **Backfist** — to temple/head, usually after lap sau (arm pull) or covering the opponent's arm.
- Strike kinetics: force starts at the foot with body rotation → hip → lower back → shoulder → release through the hand; stationary turn amplifies the strike.
- Strike “through” the target: focus on a point several centimeters BEHIND the surface.
- Favored timing: strike into oncoming movement — when the opponent's head comes forward after a pull.

## Legs

- At the first level, legs mainly provide movement. Kicks are rare: **to knee and groin**, plus trips, sweeps, and leg knocks — all low, without high kicks.
- Unweighted lead leg = tool for stopping and preemptive kicks and blocking low-level leg attacks.
- Defense against a round kick to the head: body turn + both hands up in wu sau, then immediate inward step and liver strike.

## Sticky contact (Chi Sau)

- Essence: hands “stick” to the opponent's arms; constant tangential contact reads attack timing and direction WITHOUT vision — reflexive, instant response.
- **Dan Chi Sau (one hand):** basic cycle — one holds fook sau on top, the other tan sau beneath; palm attack → downward parry → counterpunch → rise into bong sau. A role-switching attack-defense conveyor without breaking contact.
- **Poon Sau (“rolling hands,” two hands):** partners roll their forearms in circles; one has both arms in fook sau, the other bong sau + tan sau, positions roll (bong↔tan) with unchanged contact. Leader sets tempo, other follows.
- **Key dramatic beat:** broken contact or loss of pressure = instant strike into the opened zone (chest, eyes). “Felt emptiness — the hand shot out by itself.”
- Taking control of the opponent's centerline is the goal of all contact. No excess force: follow pressure, keep touching.
- **Lap Sau (cycle for sparring scenes):** wrist contact → arm grab + simultaneous straight punch with the other hand → defender neutralizes with bong sau → catches the wrist in turn, pulls down and punches → bong sau again... An endless rolling “windmill” of grab-pull-punch-wing — ideal for a dynamic medium shot.

## Siu Lim Tao — for training scenes

- “Little idea form.” Performed STANDING STILL in frontal stance, feet motionless from start to finish — only arms move. Gaze straight ahead, breathe through the nose, exhale on each strike.
- Tempo: slow, hypnotically focused (especially the first third), tension only in flashes at movement endings. Advanced variation — on one leg, raised thigh horizontal.
- Three parts: 1) tan sau + fook sau; 2) gum sau + biu sau; 3) tan/gan/bong sau.
- Visual skeleton for a shot:
  1. Opening: feet together → fists to chest → bend knees → toes apart → heels apart (stance entry).
  2. Cross arms low (left over right) → raise → fists to armpits.
  3. Slow left Yat Chi Kuen down the center → open palm upward → huen sau (wrist circle) → fist → retract to chest. Repeat right.
  4. Core (slowest): tan sau forward → huen → wu sau → fook sau to chest → forward again — THREE TIMES with each hand; elbow creeps along the centerline, stopping 15 cm from the body.
  5. Pak sau toward opposite shoulder → Yan Jeung palm strike down the center → huen → retract.
  6. Gum sau sideways-downward, behind the back, forward; arms sideways → tan sau with both → jut sau downward → biu sau with both forward-upward.
  7. Wu Jeung palm strikes, tan→gan→tan→huen→Wun Jeung combinations, bong sau→tan sau→Yan Jeung (each hand).
  8. Ending: gan sau with both → two chain Yat Chi Kuen (left-right) → huen → retract → bring foot in, hands down, exhale.

## Principle formulas (for choreographer notes)

1. **Not force against force:** deflect/let the attack pass rather than stopping it rigidly; leave the line through a step or body turn.
2. **Centerline:** strike the opponent's center, cover your own; the shortest straight line always beats an arc.
3. **Simultaneity (lin siu dai da):** block and counterstrike are ONE movement phase, zero pause; the blocking hand can itself be a strike (biu sau = eye thrust).
4. **Whole body in the strike:** foot → hip → lower back → shoulder → hand; body rotation amplifies it.
5. **Economy:** short movements without a windup, no pulling the hand back before striking; softness until contact, a split-second explosion at the end.
6. **Control the opponent's arms:** grabbing/covering one arm simultaneously restrains the second (pulling toward their centerline turns their shoulder toward you); final strike when both of the opponent's arms are “tied up.”
7. **False first strike:** first strike looks completely real → opponent reacts with a block → instant strike into the revealed zone.
8. **Outside:** when evading a strike, get OUTSIDE the attacking arm — from there the opponent has nothing with which to continue.
9. **Four defense zones:** body divided into upper-left/upper-right/lower-left/lower-right; upper zones covered by pak/tan/bong/biu/wu, lower by gan/jum; one hand high means the other must cover low.
10. **80% hands:** close-range combat; legs provide steps, occasional low kicks to knee/groin, sweeps.
