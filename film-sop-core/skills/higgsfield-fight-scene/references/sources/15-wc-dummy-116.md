# 15 — Wing Chun: 116 Dummy Techniques

**Type:** book (scan, 63 pp.) — Yip Chun, “116 Wing Chun Dummy Techniques Demonstrated by Grandmaster Yip Man” (original: Yip Chun, “116 Wing Tsun Dummy Techniques as demonstrated by grandmaster Yip Man,” HK 1981; Russian edition, Chisinau 2001). Photo stages show Yip Man himself (photographed shortly before his death) and his son Yip Chun.

**What it adds:**
- Canonical muk yan jong (wooden dummy) form of Yip Man's school: 116 movements in eight parts — direct source for TRAINING SCENES in Wing Chun / proto-JKD style (Bruce Lee learned this very foundation from Yip Man).
- Exact dummy construction as a prop: proportions, three arms, leg, suspension frame — useful for building the shot and sound.
- Applications section: each dummy pattern shown as paired fighting (A defends — counters) through photo stages — ready beats for fight choreography.
- Style logic: centerline, inside/outside gates, “one effective movement is better than many flashy ones,” simultaneous defense and attack, hands+leg on one beat.
- Historical bonus: Yip Man → Bruce Lee → Jeet Kune Do connection (Bruce did not finish learning the dummy form, returned in 1965 to complete it — a scene/story).

## The dummy as an onscreen object
- Cylindrical post ~152 cm long, ~22 cm in diameter, made from hardwood.
- **Three “arms”:** two upper arms project forward at angles (like an opponent's extended fists), a third “middle” arm sits lower, centered. **One “leg”:** bent, thick, emerges low and bends toward the floor — imitates the opponent's lead leg.
- Classic suspension: post supported on two horizontal bars passing through its body, bars supported by two vertical posts (frame by a wall). The post is slightly MOBILE and springy — on impact the dummy shudders, rocks, and “answers” with recoil. There is also a rigid version — a post buried in the ground (“jong”).
- Dummy levels = opponent's body levels: upper arms — upper level (head/chest), middle arm — middle (stomach), leg — lower (knee/shin).
- Narrative function: dummy = partner who “never tires or gets injured”; conditions the fighter's forearms and shins, teaches centerline and angle awareness. Close-ups: conditioned forearms glide over wooden arms, palm slaps the trunk.

## Working patterns (116 movements → 12 typical patterns, with phases)
Form — eight parts; almost everything mirrored left-right with body turns around the dummy. Typical patterns:

1. **Neutralization + sticky contact (bong sao → tan sao).** Phase 1: wing forearm (bong sao) meets the dummy's “arm” from above. Phase 2: hand rotates palm-up (tan sao), elbow low, contact unbroken. Phase 3: other palm strikes the body (“lower deceptive palm”). Basic combination throughout the form.
2. **Slap-block + straight strike (pak sao).** Palm sharply slaps the dummy's “arm” (inside or outside gates) — a loud, dry clap — followed immediately by a straight fist/palm strike to the trunk. Series: right-left-right pak sao in succession (movements 21–24).
3. **Double control above and below (high and low gan sao / kwun sao).** Both hands simultaneously rest on upper and middle dummy arms (one cuts high, the other low), body sideways. A refrain/“punctuation mark” throughout the form — repeated after almost every combination, with a backward step.
4. **Circular hand transition (kau sao + tok sao / huen sao).** Wrists circle around the dummy's “arms,” passing from outside gates to inside — hands seem to wrap around the wood — then a vertical palm strike.
5. **Downward pressure + vertical strike (jut sao + vertical palm).** One hand sharply presses a dummy arm downward (post nods), the other simultaneously strikes forward into the trunk with a vertical palm.
6. **Neck grab / grabbing hand.** Hand passes between dummy arms, palm rests behind the post's “head,” sharp pull toward oneself — dummy rocks on the frame. With a partner, this is unbalancing + an intercepting strike.
7. **Chopping palms (throat-cutting hand / shovel hand).** From bong sao, hand rolls into a horizontal palm-edge chop to the trunk's “throat” or a shovel-shaped strike to the “chin.”
8. **Snapping hand from fook sao.** Hand rests on dummy arm (fook sao — “bridge hand”), then quick snapping slaps onto the arm — light staccato knocking, sticky-hands training.
9. **Po-pai — double palm push.** Both palms (one fingers up, the other down) strike the trunk synchronously — frontal or side version, stepping behind the dummy's “leg.” The most “powerful” sound: a muffled double impact, dummy visibly recoils in its frame.
10. **Hand + side kick combination.** Hands maintain contact (tan sao + deceptive palm), lead leg rises and strikes: side thrust kick to “torso,” diagonal downward-sideways kick to “knee,” front pushing kick to trunk. Style key: hand and leg work on ONE beat.
11. **Leg control (foot press / sliding along the leg).** Fighter's foot slides down the dummy's bent “leg,” steps on and traps it — simultaneously the hands restrain the lower arm with low bong sao / gum sao.
12. **Cross and swinging kicks (form finale).** The only rear-leg kicks: cross pushing kick to “knee” with a forward step and a swinging turning kick, while both hands grab and pull the dummy arm. Form closes with double tok sao — both palms push upward against the undersides of the dummy arms — and withdrawal.

## Combat applications (paired breakdown section, pp. 46–105)
- Body map: midline/centerline, three levels, inside and outside gates — all blocks and strikes described relative to the gates.
- Every dummy pattern = response to a specific attack: pak sao — against straight fist (parry + straight to face); bong sao — against powerful straight attack (wing neutralizes, sidestep, foot strike to knee); kwun sao — against double strike on two levels; high-low gan sao — against round kick; gum sao — against kicks from below.
- Signature finishes: neck grab and pull → opponent falls forward → intercepting fist to face; grabbing hand at wrist + elbow control → swinging strike to lead knee + two-hand pull — opponent loses balance and falls (how Yip Man defeated a robber at the police station — ready scene, pp. 110–111).
- Tactical motto: “Stand when attacked, press toward the opponent when they retreat, and strike when the hand is free.”
- Leg principle: after a kick, the leg does NOT return backward — it immediately delivers the next strike simply by changing trajectory; all kicks use the lead leg at close range, except the side thrust kick (long range) and cross kick (rear leg).
- Economy: one effective movement protects better than many flashy ones; a weaker, slower fighter wins through technique — ideal for an “old man against a brute” scene.

## Training scene: rhythm and sound
- **Form rhythm:** three- to five-movement combination on one side → body turn → mirrored combination. Pulse: ta-ta-TA (block-transition-strike), gan sao pause-refrain, footstep — then again. Parts 1–2 slower (footwork), parts 6–8 denser (po-pai, kicks, final cascade).
- **Sound:** three textures — dry palm SLAP on wooden arm (pak sao), muffled forearm KNOCK against the forearm bar (bong/tan/gan sao), low THUD of palms/foot into trunk (po-pai, kicks) + frame creak and vibration when the dummy rocks after a “neck” pull. Staccato snapping-hand series like a drum fill.
- **Movement:** fighter barely moves away from dummy — circles it with tiny steps, both hands always touching wood, elbows low, back upright; leg rises without a windup, strikes, and immediately plants behind the dummy's “leg.”
- **Emblematic shots:** elderly master (Yip Man was filmed a couple of years before death — a thin old man in dark clothes) whips through a dummy combination without a single unnecessary pause; student repeats and falters; close-up — reddened forearms; ending — double tok sao and a backward step away from the now-still yet still-vibrating dummy.
- Story hook: in 1965 Bruce Lee visits Yip Man to finish learning the unfinished dummy form and asks to film it — Yip Man refuses; the form is photographed only for selected students. “The master's secret film” — a ready MacGuffin.
