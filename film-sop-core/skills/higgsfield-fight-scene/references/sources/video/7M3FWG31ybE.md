# Bruce Lee's Jeet Kune Do – The Backfist (7M3FWG31ybE, 8:10)
Channel: Dan Lok. Filmed outdoors (park, coniferous forest behind), Dan Lok in Bruce Lee T-shirt, partner holding small round black mitt; a student practices under coaching in the second half.

**Essence:** Three JKD backfists: simple (straight-lead mechanics), whipping/snapping (whip-like wrist, hip turns opposite way, instant next strike), and heavy finishing backfist with full-body rotation “like a baseball bat”. Backfist is Lee's favorite technique: not a finisher, but a lightning-fast stunning strike.

**Techniques by timestamp**
- 00:03–00:30 — Intro: backfist is Lee's favorite technique (Enter The Dragon, Tao of Jeet Kune Do, Bruce Lee's Fighting Method); “It's not meant to be a finish-blow. It's meant to be a quick strike that stuns the opponent”.
- 00:46–01:14 — Targets and striking surface: strike with top two knuckles, fist slightly rotated; targets temple, ear, side of neck, groin (“right there, right there” — pointing to partner's target areas).
- 01:16–02:14 — 1st backfist, simple (title “1ST BACKFIST: THE SIMPLE BACKFIST”, ~1:30): do not whip arm alone — “you're not whipping the arm… you want to use your full body”; straight-lead mechanics but with back of fist; hip drives in (“see how I'm putting my hip into it?”); reaches surprisingly far. Frames: stepping strikes to round mitt.
- 02:17–03:07 — 2nd backfist, snapping/whipping (title ~2:25): hand whips in front, hip turns OPPOSITE to first variation (“one, the hip motion is this way, the other is the opposite”); purpose — immediately follow the snap with another strike (“now I can strike with something else, very quickly”). Frames: short mitt whip then immediate other hand.
- 03:09–03:36 — 3rd backfist, heavy/finishing (title ~3:10): full torso/hip rotation — “I turn my hip. Boom… that's a very devastating strike”.
- 03:42–03:52 — Warning: do not swing a backfist from behind the back — “you'll break your bones”; use hammer fist on this trajectory (frame: wind-up over shoulder, striking with fleshy side of fist).
- 03:53–04:10 — Street scenario (movie insert with fall onto grass): “you see a blur… he comes in, boom” — whole-body hammer fist against attacker from side.
- 04:11–04:33 — Offensive use: knock away/remove opponent's weapon-hands with backfist, then hit; with conditioning, “even this thing hurts” (forearm/backfist strike to limb). B-roll: line of kung-fu students in white with flags (~4:45).
- 04:51–05:19 — Coaching (“DEMONSTRATION AND PRACTICE” title): “make sure it's not a push motion — it's like snap”; do not tense up, release energy; body supplies “snap”.
- 05:22–05:46 — Gross-motion method: exaggerate first to grasp mechanics, then “minimize and minimize”; working on counter-motion too early yields speed without power — “you hit the guy and the guy's still standing there, that's not good”.
- 05:50–06:25 — Counter-motion/retract: hand returns “like a whip”, barely leaves stance (“this is how much my hand moves”), elbow and wrist guard centerline, “stuff is ready to fire”.
- 06:34–07:17 — Heavy-backfist practice: full-body rotation; mistake “hand leaving too early”; image — “it's like a baseball bat… a fully committed action”, without thoughts of defense, “basically unblockable”.
- 07:28–08:07 — Summary: alternate all three in training, practice recovery for third; “Be water, my friend” (together with partner).

**Visual details for choreography**
- Stance: right hand forward, fist at chin; backfist starts directly from guard without wind-up — elbow forward, wrist unwinds last.
- Three movement qualities: (1) simple — linear “shot” with hip driving forward like straight punch, fist turns to back surface; (2) snapping — short circular wrist whip in front of face, torso nearly still, hip opposite — reads as “whip crack”; (3) heavy — broad 90°+ whole-body turn, weight transfer, like a sweeping bat strike.
- Distance: unexpectedly long — step reaches from outside punching distance; snapping works very close.
- Tempo: snap strikes almost unprepared, retract faster than strike; heavy is the only “broad” frame, full commitment followed by recovery pause.
- Setting: daylight park, greenery; small round mitt produces sharp cracks; coach corrects student's wrist amplitude with hand gestures.
