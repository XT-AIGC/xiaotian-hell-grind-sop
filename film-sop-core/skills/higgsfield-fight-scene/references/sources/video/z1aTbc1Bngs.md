# Bruce Lee JKD Footwork Drills Part 1 (z1aTbc1Bngs, 13:05)
Channel: Dan Lok. Format — interview lesson: Dan Lok asks questions, Sigong Octavio Quintero (theartofjkd.com) demonstrates; student Jack drills with coaching. Location — a dark gym.

**Essence:** Basic JKD footwork — why it comes from fencing, why the strong side leads, and two initial movements: step & slide (step and bring the other foot up) and push shuffle (one explosive impulse from the rear “spring” leg). The second half is live coaching: common mistakes, auditory drills (counting/claps), and broken rhythm.

**Techniques by timestamp**
- 00:46–01:26 — Theory: Lee took footwork from fencing for mobility — “he wanted mobility to be the best way to get to your opponent, or at least escape”; a stationary target takes hits. Frames: both figures in stance, “Bruce Lee Original Training Footage” inserts (~1:30–1:55, black-and-white archive).
- 01:53–02:47 — Bai Jong stance, strong side IN FRONT (unlike boxing): “he wanted the knockout punch to be in front… use this 80% of the time”; power from short range, without a wind-up.
- 03:02–03:39 — Context: boxing has 3-minute rounds, JKD is self-defense, “we wanna finish it in seconds”; mention of Wong Jack Man vs Bruce Lee.
- 03:44–04:22 — Stance basics: both hips forward, knees slightly bent, hands raised; do NOT clench the fist tightly — “closing your fist this tight is too much… just relax”.
- 04:22–05:07 — Step & slide: lead-foot step + rear-foot follow/slide; mirrored going backward. Key details on screen (strip 2): rear heel RAISED, both knees bent, feet not turned into an “L” but nearly parallel, slightly off-center. These are “two moves” — the slow teaching version.
- 05:07–06:03 — Push shuffle: rear leg = “spring board”; push off the rear foot as the front foot lifts — “it becomes one easy motion. That's one motion… bom. That's it”. Frames (strip 3): gliding travel, both feet low over the floor, torso at a constant level.
- 06:12–06:33 — Hands move with the body for momentum; hands down = “somebody's gonna go boom right across the jawline”.
- 06:56–07:07 — The image of “wheels under our feet”: do not jump up and down, “avoid the energy going up, always direct forward” — hovering, gliding at one level.
- 07:24–08:17 — Coaching Jack (barefoot): rear heel raised, forward pressure while the front foot lifts; return by pushing off the front foot. Mistakes: leaning the torso forward/backward — “it's important that you don't lean forward”, balance centered (checked from the side on screen).
- 08:24–09:10 — Corrections: rear foot slips backward — return it; knees nearly parallel, feet parallel (not an “L”); at the extreme, the lead knee slightly shields the groin.
- 09:10–09:29 — Merge two movements into one: “Now try to combine them” — from “one-two” into a single impulse.
- 09:29–10:45 — Auditory drills: commands “One!” (forward) / “Two!” (backward), then claps; react explosively — the feeling of “like a strike going forward”; motivation: “that guy's ready to hit you hard”.
- 11:03–11:36 — Images: a runner at the start (loaded knees), a tiger before attacking, an archer — “your energy is not spread apart” — drawn and ready; dominant leg forward.
- 11:36–12:10 — Broken rhythm: irregular command tempo, “One… Two-One! Two…” — timing is disrupted, reaction refined.
- 12:12–13:05 — Summary: even the simplest push shuffle is trained through tempo, broken rhythm, sound; outro: “be water my friend”.

**Visual details for choreography**
- Stance: right (strong) foot and hand forward, narrow base, spring-loaded knees, rear heel constantly raised, relaxed fists at chest/chin height.
- Footwork: two registers — teaching step & slide (visible “step + drag” of the rear leg) and combat push shuffle (one continuous glide, no vertical torso movement, feet skim the floor).
- Distance/tempo: short movements, 30–60 cm per impulse; shuttling forward and backward; auditory drills start from complete stillness and clearly read as “freeze → shoot”. Broken rhythm produces uneven, unpredictable movement — a cinematic detail.
- Setting: dark gym with exercise machines, both teachers in black, barefoot student; the coach circles and manually corrects torso/feet — ready-made “master-student” blocking.
