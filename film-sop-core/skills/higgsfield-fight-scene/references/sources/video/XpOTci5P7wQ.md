# JKD Trapping (XpOTci5P7wQ, 49:47)
Channel: rastamax1989 (reupload of the DVD “Bruce Lee's Jun Fan Gung Fu — JKD Trapping”, Ron Balicki and Diana Lee Inosanto).

**Essence:** A complete instructional DVD on Jun Fan Gung Fu / JKD trapping: Pak Sao and Lop Sao as the two basic “hands”, their cycle drills, combining the cycles into live play, and transferring everything to focus mitts. The through-line: trapping is an attribute-building drill (like skipping rope), sensitivity and responding to pressure, rather than memorized patterns.

**Techniques by timestamp:**
- 01:41 — Intro, Ron Balicki + Diana Lee Inosanto. Answer to critics: UFC/NHB has “tons of trapping” — Shamrock, Ruden, Couture; any “pat & hit” = Hand Immobilization Attack.
- 03:02 — Dan Inosanto's analogy: a drill is like skipping rope: it builds an attribute, but in a fight “you do not jump over a rope” — do not force the drill into the situation.
- 04:02 — Pak Sao (pak=slap, sao=hand). Reference point: a contact bridge between the arms (“nobody fights like this except in Enter the Dragon” — this is a training reference point). Pak Sao + Da (da=any strike: chung choy, gua choy...).
- 05:22 — Pak Sao from connection and non-connection; “throw junk” to read whether the opponent is a front-hand or rear-hand blocker. Baiting.
- 06:23 — 5 pak sao lines: (1) over the bridge — over the arm to the face; (2) split entry — between the arms; (3) low — to the body; (4) jao sao (running hand) — hook around, feint outward; (5) gua choy — backfist when the straight line is closed.
- 09:31 — Lop Sao (pulling hand): “like a car crash” — she pulls me INTO the strike, opposing motion. Discussion: grip with or without the thumb — “everything has a price; what works for you is what works”.
- 11:33 — Key: wedge up UNDER the arm, not over — over it, you get tangled; chapter “Wedge Hand Up Under Arm”.
- 12:22 — Pak↔lop combinations: feel the pressure and choose slap or pull accordingly; “kill the art to understand it”.
- 14:44 — Centerline theory: self-centerline = “a pencil sticking out of the chest”, motherline = “a thread between the heads”. Opponent's arm crosses your centerline → lop; stays inside → pak from inside (loy pak sao / noy — outside).
- 16:45 — Lop Sao Cycle: bong sao (relaxed “wing”) + chung choy, partner lops downward, forearm-to-forearm strike (also forearm conditioning, like Bruce Lee against a table). 17:35 — “screw the arm out”, do not lift with the shoulder (protects the teres minor).
- 19:04 — Woo Sao — the protective hand waits behind: “why use two hands for one block?”; the pull creates a “whistle of wind”, a whiplash effect.
- 21:44 — Lop Sao Switch: feel the grip and capture the grabbing hand (“I steal — she steals it back”), switching the cycle to the other side.
- 23:00 — Inserting Chung Choy (cross) into the cycle: high line — partner discard + bong + lop; low line (24:16) — body punch like a boxer, partner drops elbow, roll up into bong sao; the adhere principle — do not break contact.
- 26:16 — Sang Pak (double pak sao) cycle: two consecutive slaps, answer — sat sao (back chop to the throat); targets: thumb eye wipe, palm to ear/face, hand to throat. “This is not sport — this is street fighting”.
- 28:23 — Switches in double pak: track back (track/redirect the arm and counter), switch inside/outside; train “Cadence, timing, sensitivity”, both sides equally.
- 31:20 — Combination Drill: combine lop sao cycle + double pak sao cycle into live play: “make the drill a game — it will be fun, and you will get more repetitions”. 33:53 — “JKD man looks for solutions outside the box”, “ultimate thief — we steal from everyone”.
- 34:46 — Pak Sao/Bui Jee cycle: pak + bui jee (eye jab) as a wedge; safety: fingers up, “know the weapon is there; we simply are not using it”.
- 37:00 — Lop Pak cycle: prefix lop sao before pak sao; choose pak or lop by feeling the barrier.
- 38:41 — Focus Mitt Trapping (partner Joe, black T-shirt, red mitts): knock the block TO THE SIDE, not down toward yourself (“Block to the Side”); cross → he knocks it away → lop sao + backhand. Progression: cross-lop → cross-hook-cross-lop → cross-hook-cross-lop-hook-cross-hook.
- 43:06 — Woang Pak Bui Jee on mitts: rear-hand parry + eye jab; after the hook, the hand “fires” back as an eye jab. Progression: hook-eye jab → hook-cross-hook-bui jee → + cross-hook-cross.
- 46:43 — Marrying both halves: cross-hook-cross / lop sao / hook-cross-hook / woang pak bui jee / cross-hook-cross... 3-minute rounds: minute 1 slow, minute 2 faster, minute 3 “go-go-go”.
- 48:51 — Conclusion: “do not muscle through the material”, build muscle memory, keep an open mind.

- A female instructor strikes a larger man — mechanics work through structure, not strength (reference for a scene with contrasting genders).
