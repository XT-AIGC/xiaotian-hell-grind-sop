# Bruce Lee's Jeet Kune Do – Footwork (wmFPVRe44q4, 20:40)
Channel: Dan Lok (student of Sifu Tat Wong, an original student of Bruce Lee)

**Essence:** A complete catalog of basic and advanced JKD footwork: stance → forward/backward shuffle → push step → side step → pivot step → quick shift → burst → pendulum step. Filming: bright room with wooden floor, a WHITE TAPE line on the floor for stance alignment; frequent close-ups of feet; student Shin for correcting mistakes; inserts of Bruce Lee photos and a fight excerpt (~16:30).

**Techniques by timestamp**
- 00:04–01:50 — Philosophy: “The art of successful hitting and punching is the art of correct distance judging” (Bruce Lee); Tat Wong's analogy: a fighter without footwork is a tank you cannot move; Bruce closed a 6–7-foot gap — “people can't block him”. Much of JKD footwork comes from fencing (bridge the gap), but without a weapon in the hand — both speed and power generation must be addressed.
- 02:00–02:55 — JKD stance along tape: lead toes point neither straight forward nor sideways — an intermediate angle; rear heel RAISED: “It's a trigger. It's like a handgun — this is what gives us all the power and speed”. A flat rear foot means no mobility. (Frames ~110–170s: foot close-ups on the white line.)
- 02:57–04:30 — Forward/backward shuffle: rear foot stays put, short lead-foot step; closes a small gap “just to give you that extra distance to be able to hit”; backward mirrors it, “transfer my weight, and then I can go forward again”. Floor tape allows self-correction of alignment.
- 04:32–06:45 — Push step forward/backward: the same, but the rear leg FOLLOWS the lead leg (continuous movement) — “boom, and boom”; mistake — off-balance when the torso overtravels; weight 60/40 or 50/50 — “experiment with it”; distance demo: push step covers a noticeably larger gap (frames ~340–400s: long slide across the floor).
- 06:46–09:05 — Student correction (Shin): stance width ≈ shoulder width + a little; “this is no good 'cause you can't move… you gotta find that cat-like, tiger-like movement — always ready to boom” (frames ~420–540s: “DEMONSTRATING THE TECHNIQUE” overlay, Dan corrects Shin's stance from the side).
- 09:07–11:04 — Side step: same posture, moving right/left; quality check: “my alignment [is] still [a] straight line going through — that's when you know your footwork is decent”; variation with a torso slip away from a punch — “very, very fluid motion”, not a jerk.
- 11:05–13:06 — Pivot step (“THE 'PIVOT STEP'” overlay ~11:05): retreating from a tackle/grapple gets you taken down; pivot = angle around the center, right or left, even TOWARD the opponent (“earned some distance there”); partner demo: leg grab — pivot takes you off-line, “that's where I do my stuff… this way I can strike” (frames ~700–790s: partner bends to grab legs, Dan angles off and remains in striking range).
- 13:07–14:25 — Quick shift: rapid stance-side change; NOT a straight step-through (“too slow”), but an arc — “the motion is a little bit more like a curve”, protecting the centerline; shown in slow motion.
- 14:27–15:59 — Burst: longest range, combining push step + pendulum + kick (usually side kick); “Bruce can cover someone in five, six feet. Us, mere mortals — maybe three, four feet”; drill: “one, two, three” — partials without extending the leg (frames ~880–960s: long travels across the room).
- 16:16–18:45 — Pendulum step (“THE 'PENDULUM STEP'” overlay, black-and-white Bruce Lee fight insert ~16:30): “signature footwork in JKD”, pendulum like a clock — “one foot replaces the other, that's the momentum”; this is CONCEALED footwork: “you're trying to hide your intent” — opponent thinks you are standing still; unlike Muay Thai, JKD hides the kick; keep center of gravity level — no bouncing up or dipping down; hide the rear leg behind the lead leg (“hide it behind your front foot”).
- 18:45–19:35 — Pendulum + kick: break into 2–3 parts: “pendulum step — just lift your knee”, then half motion, then side kick; combination: forward shuffle → pendulum step → side kick.
- 19:36–20:40 — Conclusion: watch repeatedly, put tape down at home, “practice many, many times until it becomes like walking”; outro “Be water, my friend”.

**Visual details for choreography**
- Stance: strong leg forward, toes at ~30–45°, rear heel always raised (“trigger”), soft knees; width shoulder-width + a little; floor tape as the alignment axis — excellent prop for a training scene.
- Step hierarchy by distance: shuffle (small gap) → push step (medium) → burst (maximum, 3–4 feet for mortals, 5–6 for Bruce) — build fight escalation along this ladder.
- Pivot step against tackle: leg grab → angle around lead foot, hero remains to the side in striking position — a clean cinematic counter-grappling beat.
- Pendulum: feet switch beneath an unmoving torso, head at one level — “hidden loading” of the kick; ideal when a seemingly static hero suddenly fires a side kick.
- Tempo/movement quality: all “very fluid”, feline; contrasting student errors: torso tipping, base too wide/narrow, bouncing center of gravity.
