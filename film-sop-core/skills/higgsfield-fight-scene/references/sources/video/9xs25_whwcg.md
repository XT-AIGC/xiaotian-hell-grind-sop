# Jeet Kune Do Tutorial (9xs25_whwcg, 41:17)
Channel: rastamax1989 (reupload of the DVD “Bruce Lee's Jun Fan Gung Fu — JKD Kickboxing”, Century Vision 2004; Ron Balicki, Diana Lee Inosanto, partner Joe). A continuation of their trapping DVD (XpOTci5P7wQ).

**Essence:** The kickboxing component of Jun Fan Gung Fu: entries that bridge distance with eye jabs and feints, sidekick counters (ha pak / jeet que / jik tek), a gloved “ping-pong” counter-recounter drill, and the Jow Sao House series — transitions from running hand to takedowns and armbar. A bridge between striking and the ground game.

**Techniques by timestamp:**
- 01:41 — Intro. Diana: “uncle Bruce” created the art FOR THE STREET → train in sneakers (street shoes), not barefoot; mitts/gloves are mandatory.
- 02:39 — Left lead: traditionally JFGF teaches right lead (strong side forward), but “use your preferred side”; train both (ambidextrous).
- 03:05 — Entries: bridge the gap. Eye jab (bui jee) with full torso commitment — “be Houdini”, make them believe you are really reaching for the face. Baiting.
- 04:00 — Entry 1: bui jee → o'ou tek (round/hook kick in JFGF) → cross-hook-cross → kick to the mitt. The strike-feint opens the line for the cross.
- 06:08 — Entry 2 (ABD — Attack By Drawing): fake low punch (chap choy) → high (gua choy) → kick → cross-hook-cross → round kick. “Throw a FULL punch in the feint, not half of one”.
- 07:29 — Entry 3: combination — high-low-high → kick → cross-hook-cross → kick; partner comfort: the holder can cue with the mitt or receive the kick directly to the body (“the teacher also learns to tolerate pain”).
- 09:56 — Sidekick counters. Ha Pak (low slap): like a basketball player's dribble before a free throw — slap and IMMEDIATELY eye jab, “rebounding like a pinball”, without stopping → o'ou tek → combo.
- 12:21 — Jeet Que (jeet=interception, que=bridge): the forearm is a shock absorber; strong pressure makes it spring back, weak pressure barely moves it; then the same entry tree.
- 14:23 — Jik Tek counter: against his sidekick — jeet que + hop backward and TOE UP INTO THE GROIN (front toe kick) while retreating; “wear a cup”; if it lands, the opponent becomes “rooted”, enter directly; if not, use the usual 3 entries.
- 17:40 — Gloves (Joe): section without mitts. First half: his sidekick → ha pak, bui jee, o'ou tek, cross-hook-cross → lop sao + gua choy (backfist) → hook-cross-hook → lead-leg round kick (leg or body — “whatever I see open”).
- 20:15 — Second half: round-kick counter — shoulder roll (receive on shoulder blade/back, “roll with the punches”; roll with the low kick too, otherwise “I'll let the air out of your tire”), answer hook-cross-hook → bob & weave → woang pak bui jee (parry+eye jab) → cross-hook-cross → sidekick.
- 23:44 — Ping-pong drill: both halves connect into an endless counter-recounter cycle. “Not about speed — about cadence and rapport”; “speed makes mistakes easy to hide — slow down and become cleaner”.
- 27:38 — Takedown 1: from lop sao → figure four (the arm resembles the numeral 4): grip the wrist + your own biceps, “impact it up into my chest”, sit down → wrist lock.
- 29:32 — Jow Sao House series: cross → jao sao (running hand) around the block; pull — if he pulls back, duck UNDER the arm, ear pressed to his side (otherwise you catch an elbow during the spin-out), thumb-down hand on his hip → press him into the mat → hand across face, step over → single-leg armbar; lift his head to counter a roll-out.
- 33:51 — Transfer: the opponent DICTATES the takedown side: if he does not pull back, take him down to the other side, shoulder in his armpit. Armbar at 45°, not 90° — a tighter “crimp”, knee closing the gap, hand on his face to prevent escape.
- 36:11 — Single leg reap: jao sao → pull + shoulder strike → hook the neck, step inside, reaping throw (reaping sweep with leg lift) → step over → armbar, control the head.
- 38:11 — Series finale: the same from a sidekick entry (ha pak → immediate jao sao); if the reap fails — tuck head under the arm, step over, sit back into armbar.
- 39:54 — Outro: “be patient, do not muscle through the material; slow = clean, then your speed will improve”.
