# Bruce Lee's Jeet Kune Do - Documentary (_naZzjLzVXk, 55:35)
Official training film “Bruce Lee's Jeet Kune Do” (Legacy Productions, ~1990s, supported by the Bruce Lee Estate). Narrated by Bruce Lee himself (archive recordings) + Brandon Lee, Dan Inosanto, Ted Wong; footage of Bruce's private training, student interviews, a red-walled kwoon, a backyard with a bean bag, James Coburn as a student. Subtitles are automatic (en asr), with some distorted names/terms (“Jeet kundo”, “baijong”).

**Essence:** The only JKD “training film” assembled from Bruce Lee's private training footage and notes: philosophy (style without style, water), system structure (interception, distance), footwork (Bai Jong, step-and-slide, push shuffle, circles), punches/kicks, speed, and expressing oneself through martial arts.

**Techniques and sections by timestamp**
- 00:34–01:06 — Prologue: “not a movie on Bruce Lee... the first and only training film on Jeet Kune Do by Bruce Lee himself”; material from the private archive. Footage: archival scenes, “Chapter 1: In the beginning” titles.
- 02:01–02:47 — Bruce's voice: JKD = “the way of the intercepting fist” (“intercepting fish” in asr), system born ~1965.
- 03:58–05:58 — Wing Chun foundation and moving beyond it: trapping mixed with grappling (“32 different trapping methods”), groin strikes, “chopping fingers”; example: punch arrives — contact and redirection (trapping counter).
- 07:35–12:54 — Students on the method: sparring with karate/hapkido/judo practitioners; Bruce's sources — books on Western fencing and boxing; rejecting “Chinese” or “Japanese” ways of fighting — there is only the human way.
- 14:31–16:31 — Against style doctrines (“separate man... their own doctrines”); famous “Be water” monologue: “you put water into a cup, it becomes the cup...” (Bruce interview footage).
- 17:05–17:48 — Bruce's question: “how can I maintain lightning speed without sacrificing tremendous punching power”. Basic defense demonstrations: cross parry + simultaneous strike; “catch and go, parry and go, or do it together” (simultaneous defense and attack — JKD's core).
- 18:54–19:55 — “Filipino boxing” combinations (Inosanto): slip-cross-hook-cross, jab-parry-cross-hook; correction: “only arm and shoulders... shoulders too tight” — legs gradually engage.
- 21:40–22:31 — Flow between ranges (kwoon footage): savate approach → Wing Chun range → Western boxing → lock/takedown, “Jiu Jitsu/Aikido/Kali”. The instructor explicitly says, “that could be used in film here” — a choreographic combination.
- 22:55–24:02 — “JKD is like an iron chain with an iron ball attached”; scientific test (Hayward Nishioka): JKD punch more powerful than a classical karate punch; mechanics: “put the whole hip into it and snap it... make this into a weapon”.
- 24:43–24:55 — Key to interception: “to reach me you must move to me... your attack offered me an opportunity to intercept”; “in combat, distance is a continually shifting relationship”.
- 25:06–27:20 — FOOTWORK CHAPTER (Ted Wong, student since 1964): Bai Jong stance — rear (left) heel raised, toes turned inward; step-and-slide shuffle (forward/backward/left/right, in both stances); push shuffle — “you push off and you push back rather than taking a step”; circling/quarter turn — move against the opponent's circling movement. Footage: red gym, solo movement, board diagrams, “Chapter 4: Footwork” title, “Purpose of an attack” list.
- 27:44–29:25 — Combination kicking: distance and kick form change in combinations; “commitment kick” — driving through with the center of gravity moving forward; mistake — leaning backward; “you are rushing yourself” — form first, speed later.
- 30:21–33:22 — Bruce's audio corrections of students from film: “if you wish to train punching then punch, if you want to train kicking then kick”; on his sidekick against Ken Knudson: “moved so fast... his eyeballs were still where he was standing”; correction: “the foot at the ending position in impact is dangling”.
- 35:13–36:48 — James Coburn training (b/w footage, backyard): tension from control → “forget about it, just do it — boom, right on the target”; “make your kick into a weapon”; on initial speed: “the suddenness of your attack”; correction: do not drop the leg after the kick.
- 43:05–46:04 — Functional conditioning: running/cycling instead of isolation; bean bag drills: “kick the bean bag and give me the thrust”; correction: “don't turn your body so much when you finish — try to still face him”, timing: “not yet... not yet” (striking the returning bag).
- 47:12–48:07 — Bruce's speed (“deadly fast”); speed training: genetics + “muscle memory”, timing in thousandths of a second, reacting to a red light at the focus pad.
- 49:50–53:14 — Final chapter, “Think on these things”: martial arts as self-knowledge — barriers of fear, limits, success and failure; “The art of Jeet Kune Do is simply to simplify”; “I don't believe in systems... I found the cause of my ignorance”.
- 53:39–55:35 — Credits: Narrated by Bruce Lee; additional narrators Brandon Lee, Dan Inosanto, Ted Wong; special trainer James Coburn; dedicated to the memory of Bruce & Brandon Lee.

**Visual details for choreography**
- Three image textures: (1) archival b/w 8mm — backyard, training with Coburn, bean bag; (2) color 1990s interviews/demonstrations in the kwoon with red walls and columns; (3) handwritten pages of Bruce's notes and board diagrams — excellent inserts/cutaways.
- Bai Jong: narrow, semi-frontal silhouette, raised rear heel — a “spring”; the entire movement system relies on gliding rather than stepping: the torso floats at one level.
- Push shuffle reads as a short “push-rebound” — ideal for conveying the nervous pulse of a mid-range duel.
- Interception (jeet): the opponent's forward movement = start signal; dramatically — “the attacker brings himself onto the strike”.
- Range flow for a scene: kick range (savate entry) → trapping (Wing Chun hands) → boxing → lock/takedown — explicitly described as a film combination.
- Kick combination: first kick short/probing, “commitment kick” with visible whole-body travel; after the kick, the leg returns under control, torso keeps facing the target.
- Film tempo: long meditative monologues (water, barriers) intercut with explosive 1-2-second archival strikes — a rhythm model for a trailer/montage.
