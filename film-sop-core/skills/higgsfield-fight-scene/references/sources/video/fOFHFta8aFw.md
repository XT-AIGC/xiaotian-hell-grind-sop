# Bruce Lee JKD Blocking Techniques (fOFHFta8aFw, 10:15)
Channel: Dan Lok (demo with Octavio Quintero, JKD instructor)

**Essence:** JKD essentially has no “blocks”: instead of rigidly stopping a strike — parry/deflection (redirecting energy) and simultaneous strike. Core principle: every motion is an attack; defense is a side effect of striking. Entire video is partnered work at punching range in a small gym.

**Techniques by timestamp**
- 00:23 — Philosophy: JKD is an “assault / offensive art”, aim to end fight as quickly and directly as possible; “hitting on every single motion”, no block as such.
- 01:04 — Block = “rigid stop” (stopping momentum with force) — NOT what we do. Instead parry — “divert the energy / divert the pressure”. Frames: partner throws straight, other softly redirects arm inward with palm while maintaining stance.
- 01:53 — Counterexample: rigid block creates an “opening” — Dan immediately “fires right to the body”. Demo: block → instant body strike under arm.
- 02:03 — Deflecting on the inside: strike slides around blocking arm — “he just went around it”. Water metaphor: “You're going around that rock… flowing through, finding cracks”.
- 03:12 — Parry mechanics: short motion, “don't leave the perimeter of the body” — hand redirects strike only enough to pass, then counters. This is “one-two motion” — already a compromise.
- 03:29 — Ideal is single beat: not parry-then-hit, just hit. “My block is actually the arm… it's the hit”. Instead of “one-two”, “one, boom”: own centerline straight simultaneously deflects opponent's strike with forearm.
- 04:07 — Body mechanics: Wing Chun structure — elbow in/down. If elbow up as in typical jab, you get hit yourself. Hence vertical or slightly rotated fist.
- 04:27 — Arm length and base: longer arm and better base win exchange; forward step “dissipates” opponent's arm; control arm “on the way in”.
- 04:55 — Against rear punch: same idea — “My block is this sword” (forearm edge), variations: over top for leverage or inside to redirect; inside/outside angles against both hands: “here, here, here or here”.
- 05:52 — Key mindset: do not think “I am deflecting” — think “I am hitting”; deflection happens by itself. “It's still a hit”.
- 06:20 — Low strikes (low level, uppercut/low punch): redirect down/in and immediately return with strike; with good base you can “meet with him” — meet simultaneously (stop-hit logic).
- 07:02 — Four corners: Wing Chun gates (high/low gate, front/rear) face front; JKD “blades” torso — semi-side-on stance shifts angles.
- 07:37 — Training drill: partner “feeds” centerline straights, defender uses cross parry while aiming response at nose; work slowly, both hands at once: main hand deflects, other is backup.
- 08:49 — Progression: first straight punches (rare in reality but establish foundation), then curved — same motion + off-step (side step) and follow-up.
- 09:45 — Conclusion: “It's always an attack”. The longer fight continues (“one, two, three”), the higher the risk.

**Visual details for choreography**
- Location: cramped gym — green rope on wall, Canadian flag, bags, exercise balls; two men almost always face-to-face in static medium two-shot.
- Distance: constant extended-arm striking range, no gaps; all hand work within body silhouette — VERY short movements, parry amplitude 10-15 cm.
- Stance: bladed, lead hand leads, elbows down, vertical fist on centerline.
- Tempo: slow explanation → sudden fast single beat (“one, boom”) — good “frozen conversation/explosion” contrast for a scene.
- Key choreography pattern: defender's strike travels THROUGH opponent's attack on centerline, deflecting it with forearm — both movements on one beat.

**Quotes**
- “In all actuality, there is no blocking”.
- “A block feels like a rigid stop… we divert the energy”.
- “My block is actually the arm. It's the hit”.
- “You're not thinking "I'm gonna deflect". You're thinking about hitting. It happens to deflect”.
- “It's not to block, it's to hit. Find an opening and just be like water”.
