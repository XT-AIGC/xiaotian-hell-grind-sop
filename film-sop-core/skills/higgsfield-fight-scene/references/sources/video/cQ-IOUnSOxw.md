# Bruce Lee JKD Jab (cQ-IOUnSOxw, 11:14)
Channel: Dan Lok. The same dark gym as the footwork series: Dan Lok (Bruce Lee T-shirt) + Sigong Octavio Quintero (white focus mitt/glove), student Jack drills in the second half.

**Essence:** How the JKD jab differs from the straight lead: lead — full extension with the entire body while advancing toward the opponent; jab — “whip and reach”, a whip with an arc at the very end of the trajectory. Power travels along the chain from rear foot → hip → shoulder → hand; the finish is a “crack” on the target and instant return. Second half — coaching Jack: a static stance without bouncing, isolated mechanics, clap drills for speed.

**Techniques by timestamp**

- 00:59–01:34 — Jab: the same, but “this arcs at the very end” — a whipping arc at the finish; Octavio exaggerates the arc on screen (“if I wanted to exaggerate it, it does this”), the actual curve is barely noticeable.
- 01:34–02:13 — Warning about lead mechanics: striking with only “front power” uses one piece of the pie; against incoming mass, “absorb that against your shoulder” — realignment, power, advancement are needed for shocking power.
- 02:13–02:38 — Jab = “a whip and a reach”, applied relative to the opponent's current position; on sparring: “if you're not even ready to use these tools, I would not spar”.
- 02:38–02:59 — Key mechanics: keep the elbow in; “use the momentum or the acceleration off the rear foot”, push + hip engagement = whip at the very end.
- 03:00–03:41 — The “secret” (Dan Lok: “watch this 10 times”): it is not the punch — “it's the stance and the footwork”; rear leg is a loaded spring; the chain shown point by point: foot → hip (“see the hip? boom-boom-boom, it's three points, four points”) → shoulder → hand.
- 03:42–04:25 — “Crack the target” and retract; incorrect jab demonstration (“just do it wrong”) — arm by itself, full extension, torso exposed; correct version — “gouge… crack”, everything simultaneous, “boom, and then it comes right back”.
- 04:27–05:01 — Isolation for beginners: hip separately, leg separately; mistake — rotating only the shoulders (“people start twisting here”) — the center turns sideways, losing the left hand's follow-through.
- 05:04–05:29 — Goal: “hit behind the target” (strike through/beyond the target), retract into stance = quick recovery; mistakes: hand drops down, “slow recovery”, “wide” (opening up). “Crack is best”.
- 05:42–06:02 — Rapid fire: each following strike is “hitting behind the first punch” — boom, this one's coming, boom.
- 06:05–06:43 — Safety and progression: whipping back can strain a muscle — warm up; exaggeration is allowed initially, then “Point A to Point B… very short” — power over a short range of motion; “easy to generate power from here all the way, but with this much distance — it's not easy”.
- 06:44–06:59 — An honest observation: many JKD practitioners have “not enough substance behind the techniques”; “Bruce can hit someone very hard with the straight lead. But that's Bruce.”
- 07:00–08:15 — Jack on the mitt (stationary, no step): “don't bounce… be completely standing still like a rock, then deliver the hit”; first hand, then hip, then leg; “don't worry about hitting hard — focus on doing it right. You do it right, you'll hit hard”.
- 08:15–08:57 — Start with the hand at the chest to counter telegraphing (drawing back before striking); commands: “Relax. Relax. Relax… pause in between, focus”.
- 08:58–10:08 — Auditory drill: strike on a clap; “don't anticipate”; greater difficulty — the person clapping is outside the striker's field of vision (“if he cannot see Sifu, it's harder”); “no bouncing”.
- 10:10–11:14 — Training partner's role: mirror/instructor, feedback (“your hand is dropping”, “you turn too much”, “you bounce too much”); difference from boxing — vertical fist; “be water, my friend”.

**Visual details for choreography**
- Stance: right hand forward, elbow tucked in, rear leg loaded; torso still as a rock before striking — zero bounce (the opposite of boxing's springing motion).
- Strike movement: almost invisible short range of motion, final wrist “snap” with a micro-arc over the last centimeters; the hand retracts faster than it shoots out; on screen the strike reads only through the mitt and shoulder recoil.
- Kinetic chain for animation: rear foot → hip → shoulder → hand, “3-4 points” with an upward wave; incorrect versions (for contrast in a scene): sweeping motion, hand dropping down, shoulders turning.
- Distance/tempo: stationary work at arm's length from the mitt; series of jabs — each “behind” the previous one, stroboscopic rhythm; clap drill — a start from dead stillness on sound.
- Setting: dark gym, Octavio's white mitt as a bright target on screen; coach clapping behind/outside the student's field of vision — ready-made blocking for “blind reaction”.
