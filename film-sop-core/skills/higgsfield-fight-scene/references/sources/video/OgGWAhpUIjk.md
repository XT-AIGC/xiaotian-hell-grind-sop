**Techniques by timestamp:**
- 00:00 — The JKD stance is “really unique”, unlike boxing: the most powerful hand/leg is FORWARD. The classic objection is “the lead hand/leg has no power”; the answer is that it does if you understand alignment.
- 00:52 — Power formula: just two elements — Mass × Velocity. Mass = body weight: move the body correctly to increase force; add speed to increase impact. Hence the importance of stance: “the stance is already aligned BEFORE you throw the punch”.
- 01:55 — Story: John Little found a page of Bruce Lee's notes in the library — the question “what matters most?” and a penciled answer: “Footwork, stance, coordination, alignment” — “the page had been hidden all those years, but the key had been written down 20+ years earlier”.
- 03:33 — Straight-punch mechanics: from fist through shoulder to rear leg — “one solid straight line”; the whole body supports the punch from behind.
- 03:58 — Rifle analogy: brace the stock against the shoulder (a support triangle), do not hold it unsupported — the torso similarly “backs up” the punch. Self-demonstration: the arm as the “stock”, the shoulder as its support.
- 04:18 — “For every action — equal opposite reaction”: the punch pushes off and returns; correct stance = “spend less and get more” — minimum energy, maximum power (economy-of-motion philosophy).
- 04:58 — Counterexample: turn the foot/torso as in a boxing jab and support is lost, “not going to support”; a small angle adjustment makes a big difference in power. Demonstration with a student (~225–315s in the frames: Ted corrects the stance of a student in a gray T-shirt, checking the line with his hand).
- 05:27 — Sequencing rule: “NEVER move the foot first — the HAND has to move first”. The hand starts before the foot (subtle/non-telegraphic), but power comes from footwork: push off the ground, “power comes up from the ground, goes back to the ground”.
- 05:52 — Origin: “all of this comes from fencing”; the JKD punch is not trained like a boxing punch. If the foot lands before the punch, power is lost: the hand must arrive before/with the landing.
- 06:26 — Conclusion: the order is “hand first, foot pushes, land after impact” — a “very important procedure”.
