# Bruce Lee's Speed Punching Exercise - Fist Of Fury Explained (RTuGiLKA1I8, 6:08)
Channel: Dan Lok — interview/demo with a Sifu (JKD instructor); bright studio with panoramic windows and a city view, both dressed in black, working with a partner and a focus mitt.

**Essence:** Breakdown of the JKD lead punch (“fastest lead punch attack”) — JKD's most important lead-hand strike. Main points: do not rotate the torso completely (vulnerability + half the body left out of the punch), enter with the whole body and drop the weight at impact, strike like a “whip” with an immediate return.

**Techniques by timestamp**
- 00:04–00:29 — Introduction: the lead punch is JKD's primary strike because JKD puts the strong side forward (“front: lead hand, lead foot”). It is also the most misunderstood: “flick or push? Where does the power come from?”
- 00:42–01:05 — Mistake No. 1: full torso rotation (a boxing twist). Problems: the low line opens (“vulnerable down here”), only the “extension of the shoulder” works, and only half the body goes into the punch.
- 01:13–01:28 — Missing while over-rotating leaves the head, flank and low line open; you lose “levels” and “eyesight” (your eyes leave the opponent). Frames: Sifu demonstrates an over-rotated silhouette that loses its line of sight.
- 01:30–02:00 — Correct mechanics: “propel forward” — enter forward with the entire torso; on contact, “you drop the weight”; “dragging the other foot in” — draw the rear foot up rather than twisting away; power through “the right hip”.
- 02:13–02:41 — Weight transfer: almost invisible externally (“you just gotta feel it”); the punch travels over “short amount spaces” — a short acceleration distance, with the silhouette remaining rooted; “energy is coming in” forward, not sideways.
- 02:43–02:52 — Advantage: you can still “change it up” if the opponent slips, and the second strike is faster. An over-rotated punch is “one shot” with no continuation.
- 02:53–03:16 — The long, fully extended punch is only for sparring distance and “magazine posing”: “it's extended, way too extended”.
- 03:23–03:44 — Analysis of a Bruce Lee photo: even when “posing”, he uses his body and the “root of the heel” — heel placement and rooting, rather than an outstretched arm.
- 03:49–04:33 — Hand and fist: the hand always stays centered; strike with the “three bottom knuckles” (the three lower knuckles, JKD vertical fist). Mechanics: “a whip: once you make impact you arc, and it comes right back” — an arc after impact, like “gouging” upward along the opponent. A short “Boom” at the target without overcommitting: “that's all I need — just enough to get in and follow up”.
- 04:41–05:24 — Speed drill: exaggerated movement — power from the rear leg, “learn to whip the punch”. The other hand draws back in opposition: “it's like you're ripping paper when you're hitting” (the hands tear a sheet in opposite directions).
- 05:26–05:56 — Method: 10 repetitions of each variation, changing foot position and side; during learning, “telegraphing — I don't care”; gradually shorten the motion and move closer.
- 05:57–06:04 — Footwork is the foundation (reference to a separate footwork video).

**Visual details for choreography**
- Stance: semi-frontal (JKD on-guard), strong hand forward, rear heel raised; the torso does NOT rotate fully — shoulders remain almost square.
- Punch: vertical fist, trajectory strictly along the centerline; visually, a short “shot” with a whole-body step and a visible settling (drop) at impact.
- Return: the hand rebounds in an upward/backward arc faster than it traveled forward — “whip” — a whipping pattern ideal for fast editing.
- Footwork: push-off from the rear leg + drawing up the rear foot (drag), no pivot on the ball of the foot.
- Two-person dramatic contrast: the “wrong” over-rotated punch (beautiful, cinematic, long) versus the “right” short, rooted punch — a ready-made posing/effectiveness contrast (reference to “magazine posing”).
- “Ripping paper” drill: both hands moving in opposition — a dynamic, readable gesture for a training scene.
- Location/filming: static camera, medium shot, daylight through panoramic windows, city skyline in the background; focus-mitt work in some frames.
