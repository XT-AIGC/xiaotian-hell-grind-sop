# Bruce Lee's Jeet Kune Do Trapping Techniques - Lap Sao (ad17nEj_TBg, 5:11)
Channel: Dan Lok (demonstrating with partner Shin, backyard, daylight, wooden fence behind)

**Essence:** Analysis of Lap Sao (“pulling hand”) — a JKD trapping technique: pull opponent's arm down/sideways while striking with the other hand. Key: power comes from torso rotation, not the arm; the aim is to unbalance the opponent, not simply “remove” his arm.

**Techniques by timestamp**
- 00:01 — Intro: Lap Sao vs Pak Sao. Lap Sao = pulling hand, Pak Sao = slapping/pressing hand. Both appear in Bruce Lee films, but “cinema ≠ application”.
- 00:26–00:45 — When Pak Sao fails against stiff resistance (“give me full force”), redirect opponent's pressure: instead of pushing, pull along his force vector. Frames: forearms in contact, Dan catches wrist and strips arm downward.
- 00:50–01:11 — Mechanics: do NOT pull with the arm (“look at the muscle — it doesn't work”). Pull elbow toward your torso + rotate body. Frames: pronounced hip turn, elbow drawing to ribs.
- 01:11–01:31 — Synchronization: pull + straight punch with other hand simultaneously, opposing motion “like two trains hitting together”. Not “pull, then strike” — one integrated motion.
- 01:36–01:53 — Post-pull control: partner cannot escape (“Shin, try to move”), opponent's arm sealed across his own torso.
- 02:00–02:20 — Mistake: perform Lap Sao and remain neutral face-to-face — 50/50 exchange (“he can hit me, I can hit him”). Correct: pull opponent off balance, turn his torso, open the line.
- 02:25–02:36 — Footwork: body rotation mandatory; without it both remain on the firing line.
- 02:40–03:09 — Best variation: against opposite stance (opponent in left lead). Pull the far arm — your head leaves the line, opponent's free hand is far away; “bone mechanic” (bone structure, not muscle). After pull, opponent's entire flank is open.
- 03:09–03:46 — Partner drill: alternating Lap Sao left/right, partners exchange roles. Mistake — a “windmill” of wasted excessive force (“you see a lot of Wing Chun guys do this all day long”). Pulls short and sharp, preserving structure.
- 03:55–04:33 — Wooden dummy: if your drill moves the wooden dummy, you can move a person. After arm strip: strike, chop to the neck — shown at ~30% force; full version with torso rotation can “send a guy flying”.
- 04:42 — Outro: “practice” — technique lives only through repetition.

**Visual details for choreography**
- Distance: trapping range — forearms in constant contact, chests ~50-70 cm apart.
- Stance: narrow, semi-frontal, hands on centerline (JKD's Wing Chun inheritance); weight slightly on rear leg before pull.
- Key pull visual: pulling elbow comes to own ribs, hips and shoulders rotate 45-90°, opponent “nods” forward/down, his shoulder turns across his torso.
- Simultaneity: pull with one hand + punch with the other in one beat — visually reads as “scissors”.
- Drill tempo: cyclic, metronomic (~1 pull per second), sudden explosive accents demonstrating the fighting version.
- Partner after correct Lap Sao — broken silhouette: forward lean, lost axis, free hand cannot reach target.
- Filming: static medium shot, both fighters at three-quarter angle, neutral background — movements read through silhouettes.
