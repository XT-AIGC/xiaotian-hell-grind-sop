## 1. Stance in motion

**Two-stage YJKYM entry (Hi29C5sOJsQ)** — the stance's most cinematic beat: neutral, feet together → pivot on heels, toes outward “as wide as possible” → pivot on balls of feet, heels outward → settle: toes INWARD, knees bent and drawn together. Reads as the fighter “clicking” into a platform — two clicks and a statue.
- “Sheep clamping” (Kim Yeung Ma): all force inward — knees/thighs clamp an imaginary sheep; pelvis tucked, “seated” posture (sit on it), back vertical, shoulders lowered. Can sit for hours — the form is performed in this posture.
- KEY: this is NOT a fighting stance (“groin open, throat open — you don't fight like this”), but a “weighted vest”: one learns to EXPLODE from it. For scenes: YJKYM = training/meditative shots; hero transitions into combat with an explosive step/turn.
- Fighting stance (n-crGQ3NWNs): shoulder-width or slightly wider, knees slightly bent, “coiled” — compressed spring; man sau (lead) + wu sau (rear shield palm at chin) guard, elbows down, “pyramid” before sternum.
- Typical errors — ready choreography for “untrained” opponents: torso tilted forward/backward, stance spreading into a wide karate horse stance, wandering center of gravity; elbow spills outward → push sweeps away entire body (xsXAK3DtbDc: Shin presses — Dan stands rooted, “novice” moves away).
- Stance-scene rhythm: prolonged stillness + tiny pivot movements → sudden explosion. Silhouette contrast (AVPavzLbTVk): boxer — wide stance, gloves at cheekbones, torso windup; Wing Chun practitioner — narrow frontal stance, bare hands at center.

## 2. Hands in motion

Universal law of all hands (throughout Dan Lok): movement FROM THE ELBOW, not the hand (“focus on the hand — amateur”); elbow — fixed pivot a fist's distance from center (fixed elbow, 7ic3Y9Jh3VE); shoulder relaxed; every “block” is an attack forward along the centerline; block→strike conversion without resetting position.

- **Wu sao (D5XHHW_ut0I):** vertical forearm at center, open palm angled toward opponent's face, fingers tightly together (“no lazy fingers”), thumb tucked — exposed finger gets grabbed and broken (tiny joint-lock beat for the “victim”). Formula “Center line, elbow, push.” Conversion to strike — frame to frame: palm closes into fist + elbow extends = strike without windup. One wu sao neutralizes an entire straight blast with tiny shifts.
- **Bong sao (qyIXOinBHfM + n-crGQ3NWNs):** NOT a “chicken wing” flap — elbow barely moves, forearm rotates around it, fingers “touch an imaginary wall” and point toward throat. Angle ~45°, elbow does not cross centerline. “Spring power” — constant forward pressure, elbow “thrusts” into opponent. A last resort — the losing fighter's refined flinch reflex. Torso rotation essential. Most cinematic block: outward forearm roll — bridge into lap sao/trap.
- **Tan sao (xsXAK3DtbDc):** supinated “plate” palm, thumb tucked, forearm ~45°, goes NOT sideways but INTO CENTER — wedges into the punch THROUGH attacker's arm + simultaneous response from other hand (tan da). “This is an attack, not a defense.” Finisher from contact: coin-toss slap — hand flicks like a coin, without windup.
- **Biu sao (mfj16bcXlNk):** spear hand, “all eyes and throat”; faster than a fist, several inches longer; travels straight OR in an S-curve around a block — “snakelike” hand movement; chains of 2–3 biu with angle changes (top→side→top), each starts at previous deflection point — a wave without hand retraction. “Assassination art,” small fighter's weapon against large.
- **Fook sao** (n-crGQ3NWNs): bent-wrist block, key to chi sao. **Pak sao:** palm-heel slap with a snap, best first-defense option (Dan Lok prefers pak/lap to bong+lap — “looks good in films, but three movements against one punch”). **Jum sao:** tiny diagonal downward chop, body turn adds range. **Gan/guan sao (xI1Uxxke7CY):** downward elbow-driven drop “toward the pocket” against uppercuts and kicks — “not catching the strike, dropping it” (drop, not catch), large sweeping arc forgives mistiming (“a real fight is a blur”). **Huen sau:** wrist circles opponent's arm → immediate palm thrust.
- Conversions (nW7sOO82fk8): “punch instantly becomes block, block becomes punch” with the same limb without pause; palm jerk — pull arm toward yourself → intercepting strike (“pull in and meet”); bong sao → double palm push — opponent flies back.

## 3. Sticky contact and chi sao

- Chi Sau (nW7sOO82fk8) — calling card: two fighters face to face, forearms glued, hands rolling in a tan-bong-fook wave, torsos almost still — a “conversation with hands.” Teaches instinctive reading of force direction and redirection with minimum movement.
- **Dan Sau cycle (single sticky hands):** partner A: tan sao → low palm thrust → bong sao; partner B: jum sao → straight punch → fook sao; fook rests on tan, cycle rolls with a “firm yet relaxed flow of force” — ready loopable pattern for a training scene.
- Pressure-based correction (qyIXOinBHfM): Sifu silently presses arm — student adjusts angle; “too much up, too high”; opening = instant light slap to chest/throat. “He didn't tell me — it's all sensitivity.” Bodies sway under pressure when structure is wrong — beat marker “structure breaks → torso collapses.”
- Structure test (xsXAK3DtbDc, D5XHHW_ut0I): centimeter-scale angle adjustment instantly changes stability; partner punches at full power — tan sao holds without flinching; “not force — mechanics, my angle is good.”
- Stickiness in combat (AVPavzLbTVk, Szs3-z91iKQ): after first touch, opponent's arm is NOT released — pin/pull (lap), inward slide into control, entry into dead zone beside gloves.
- Layered relaxation (xsXAK3DtbDc): fingers → forearm → shoulder → hip → torso; “like bamboo — springy, not tense.”

## 4. Kicks and footwork

- All kicks (n-crGQ3NWNs) — fast, LOW, straight, without windups, never above torso: groin, knees, shins; role — “harassment” and closing distance, always supporting hands (kick+punch on one beat). Front heel kick straight from ground with knee snap; cross stomp kick with sole breaks balance for finish; low side kick — stomping heel. Combinations: cross stomp → side kick to torso; opponent's front kick → block → sweep with their own force.
- Kick defense (xI1Uxxke7CY): guan sao “cuts” shin down-sideways, torso vertical, distance maintained; do NOT strike against force — redirect it.
- **Shuffle** (n-crGQ3NWNs): lead foot ~15 cm, rear follows — fighter “drives into” opponent with small sliding steps (chain punches + advancing step = opponent retreats straight, “sewing machine”).
- **Circle stepping:** rear foot slides forward in arc, stance change — cutting off retreating opponent's angle.
- **Shifting** (7ic3Y9Jh3VE): torso rotates around centerline axis — defensively rolls off strikes, offensively gains new angles + increased reach; power from waist/hip twist. Shifts inserted between combinations as angle changes, not retreats.

## 5. Street scenarios

- **Intercepting boxer's jab (AVPavzLbTVk):** gloved opponent throws jab/cross from range — meet the entry: parallel parry (pak/tan) + simultaneous straight to face (lin sil die dar, one beat, not block-then-strike). Long boxing distance collapses to close range, where gloves are useless; cutting angle, sticky wrist control, chain burst.
- **Quiet hand → intercept → counter (Szs3-z91iKQ):** lead hand “hovers” motionless at center — does not twitch at feints, responds only to actual trajectory; at impact a short hand rise (10–15 cm deflection, not sweeping block) + counterstrike on same count over attacking arm; then shuffle right in, neck/shoulder control, short point-blank strikes. Total calm → explosion — model broken rhythm.
- **Headlock (K1F-vXRFgyM):** break hold → trap both arms → chain straights; partner bent double, head/arm control from side, finish from above.
- **Chest push:** push dissipated by parry/body turn, counterpush (double palm strike) topples. **Wrist/sleeve grab:** wrist roll + counterstrike. **“Wild” zombie-style swing:** interception + straight to face. **Rear bear hug (nW7sOO82fk8):** double backward palm strike → throw.
- Wong-style center control: almost every response begins with palm to chin/face (head tips back, torso buckles backward) or parry toward center, then advancing chain punches; Wong “hangs” on opponent's arms between strikes.
- **Master Wong's “cinematic” reactions — a distinct presentation style:** partners EXAGGERATEDLY fly back 2–3 steps, bend double, throw heads back; every technique is a street-conflict mini-scene, not a drill. Use when readable strike “movie physics” are needed; for realism — Kevin Lee's restrained reactions.

## 6. Training scenes

- **Wall-bag (n-crGQ3NWNs):** fist and palm strikes to wall bag — “feeling penetration,” knuckle conditioning; slowly and progressively; classic Wing Chun gym feature.
- **Siu Lim Tau form:** performed extremely slowly from stationary YJKYM — hypnotic one-hand movements with static torso (reference: white background with giant calligraphy). Yip Man practiced tan sao for an hour as meditation (xsXAK3DtbDc). Editing template: solo form gesture (slow) → same with partner (sharp) — “form → application.”
- **Paired drills:** guan sao → punch cycle (one drops, other strikes); free flow at 1/3 power (“just flowing”); dan sau cycles; pressure tests. Pad work (AVPavzLbTVk): partner holds round focus pad — chain straights with forward step, linear blast.
- Period dress codes: VHS classic — master in black kung fu uniform, student in white shirt, static camera, clean silhouettes; modern — black T-shirts in loft with panoramic windows (Dan Lok), tracksuits outdoors/on waterfront (Wong), puzzle-mat gym (Lee).
- Sound design: sharp palm-on-forearm slaps — characteristic Wing Chun sound; chain series = even machine-gun burst.

## 7. Timing/tempo for the animator

- **Two modes, always contrasted:** teaching/preparation — drawn-out, “silky,” meditative; application — explosive 0.3–1-second impulse without windup. No medium tempo.
- **One beat = defense + strike.** Block and counterstrike occur on ONE count (lin sil die dar); three movements against one strike mark “cinematic unreality” (Dan Lok's criticism of bong+lap). Hand retracting after a punch automatically blocks.
- **Broken rhythm:** stationary pause (hands “silent,” quiet hand) → 2–3 movements in half a second → another stop. Master Wong: conversational pause → 1–2-second explosion → stop.
- **Chain punches:** alternating “piston” vertical fists along one tubular path, elbows down, wrist snap at end, small advancing step every 2–3 punches; even tempo without accents, 4–6 punches per second.
- **No windups:** strike starts at chest/contact point (one-inch, coin-toss slap); power from structure and body twist, not range of motion. Any broad windup/sideways chopping block is the “untrained” opponent's language.
- **Distance:** everything decided within a bent arm's length; forearms almost constantly touching; close distance with a 15 cm shuffle, not a jump.
- **Structure-failure markers:** body displaced by push, torso collapses behind hands, fingers splayed and caught — animate defeat through structural breakdown, not “flying away” (unless in Master Wong mode).
