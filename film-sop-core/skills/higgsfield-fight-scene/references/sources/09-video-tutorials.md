# 09 — Video tutorials (23 videos, reviewed through storyboards + transcripts)
**Type:** primary video sources (Dan Lok/Octavio Quintero, Ted Wong, Ron Balicki DVD, Sifu George Hajnasr, fightTIPS, Elite Academy, and others)
**What it adds to choreography:** living DYNAMICS absent from books: how a strike actually “snaps” and retracts (retraction faster than extension), what dropping the weight looks like at impact, how legs switch beneath a motionless torso (pendulum), how a steady hypnotic trapping-drill cycle suddenly breaks into an explosion. Also actual speeds and pauses (2 slow demonstrations → 2–3 at “normal speed” → reset), partners' hit reactions (head dip, broken silhouette, “rooting”), and the full texture of training scenes: mitts, wooden dummy, floor tape, clap drills, isometric straps. This is animation reference—books provide “what,” videos provide “how it moves.”

## 1. Strike mechanics in motion—what videos show that books do not

- **Straight lead—“slide + snap”**: hand fires FIRST, body follows through rear-leg push, foot lands AFTER impact (“hand first, foot pushes, land after impact”—OgGWAhpUIjk, Ted Wong). Fist–shoulder–rear-foot line like a taut string (“one solid straight line,” analogy of rifle braced against shoulder). Power = mass × velocity; “power comes up from the ground, goes back to the ground.”
- **WITHOUT torso rotation**: principal anti-boxing JKD marker. Full twist = “only half of your body into the punch” + open lower level + loss of eyesight (RTuGiLKA1I8). Correct: body “propel forward,” on impact “you drop the weight”—visible sinking, rear foot dragged up, shoulders remain almost square. Overtwisted long punch—“magazine posing.”
- **Whip / crack / retract**: strike as whip: “once you make impact you arc, and it comes right back” (RTuGiLKA1I8); jab = “a whip and a reach” with a tiny arc in final centimeters, “crack the target—and it pulls back” (cQ-IOUnSOxw). Return ALWAYS faster than extension—the key animation beat. Animation kinetic chain: rear foot → hip → shoulder → hand (“boom-boom-boom, three points, four points”). Series: each next strike “hitting behind the first punch”—stroboscopic rhythm.
- **Vertical fist**, three bottom knuckles, elbow inward-down; torso motionless before strike “like a rock”—zero bounce (opposite of boxing springiness) (cQ-IOUnSOxw, fOFHFta8aFw).
- **“Two trains” / scissors**: lop sao—one hand pulls + other punches in one beat, opposed movement “like two trains hitting together”; pull driven not by hand but elbow toward ribs + torso turn; opponent “dips” forward-down, broken silhouette (ad17nEj_TBg). Related gesture—“ripping paper”: hands tear a sheet in opposite directions (RTuGiLKA1I8).
- **3 backfist types** (7M3FWG31ybE): (1) simple—linear, straight-lead mechanics, back of fist, hip forward, reaches from unexpectedly long distance; (2) snapping—hand whip before face, hip in OPPOSITE direction, instant transition to next strike; (3) heavy/finishing—full torso rotation “like a baseball bat,” fully committed, “basically unblockable,” followed by recovery pause. Never swing from behind the back (“you'll break your bones”)—use hammer fist there. “Not a finish-blow—a quick strike that stuns.”
- **No telegraphing**: zero windup/pullback, “the hand moves before anything else” (AWHjwZdCeDg); method—exaggerate first (gross motion, “telegraphing—I don't care”), then “minimize and minimize” (7M3, RTu).
- JKD punch measured as stronger than classical karate punch; “put the whole hip into it and snap it” (_naZzjLzVXk, Nishioka test).

## 2. Footwork in motion

- **Bai jong stance**: strong side forward, narrow high base (shoulder width + a little), front toe at ~30–45°, springy knees, rear heel RAISED—“It's a trigger. It's like a handgun” (wmFPVRe44q4). Fists relaxed. A discovered Lee note: “Footwork, stance, coordination, alignment” (OgGWAhpUIjk).
- **Step & slide vs push shuffle** (z1aTbc1Bngs, _naZzjLzVXk): step & slide—training “two moves” (front step + rear drag); push shuffle—single combat impulse from rear “spring board” leg: “one motion—bom. That's it.” Reads as push-rebound, a duel's nervous pulse.
- **Hovering / “wheels under our feet”**: torso glides at one level, “avoid the energy going up, always direct forward”—feet skim floor, head does not travel vertically (z1a, wmF).
- **Step-distance hierarchy** (wmFPVRe44q4): shuffle (small gap, 30–60 cm) → push step (medium) → burst (maximum: push step + pendulum + side kick; “Bruce—5–6 feet, mere mortals—3–4”)—ready fight-escalation ladder.
- **Pendulum step** (ClfYe9Uly28, wmF): “signature footwork JKD”—legs switch UNDER torso, “like two balls hit each other,” center of mass steady; STEALTHY footwork—“you're hiding your intent”: hero outwardly still, suddenly fires side kick. Two-part “one-two” rhythm: retreat = already loaded kick (“I dodged it—and I have a wound-up kick right there”), rebound-fire without pause.
- **Side step / pivot step / quick shift** (wmF): side step preserves alignment; pivot against tackle—angular evasion around front leg, hero remains to side in striking position (cinematic countergrappling beat); quick shift—stance switch along an arc, not by stepping across.
- **Broken-rhythm drills** (z1aTbc1Bngs): sound/clap response “One!.. Two-One! Two…”—start from complete stillness, broken unpredictable motion pattern; images: runner at starting line, tiger, archer. SAA: 45° step-off + simultaneous strike—“everything has to happen at one time” (AWHjwZdCeDg).

## 3. Trapping catalog

- **27 Gates** (VxmkjF38FeI, Hajnasr; 27 honors Lee's birthday): gate = “door” from hand contact (touching hands): if a strike is blocked, hand does not retract but immobilizes block and continues striking. Upper/middle/lower/outside-gate entries; centerline targets (eyes, throat, solar plexus, groin, knee). Core—Gate 1: pak sao + punch + retract; then lop sao + gua choy, inside bong sao, low-high PIA feint, “the leak” (inverted fist through gap), jao sao (running hand—small circle around elbow), elbow, pulling headbutt, uppercut from pin, knee from palm-to-face pin, Gate 27 finale—endless re-trap hit chain “until he's done.” Demonstration rhythm: 2 slow + 2–3 “normal speed”—“tap-tap-EXPLOSION-pause.” “Punch felt before it is seen.”
- **Pak sao / Lop sao mechanics** (XpOTci5P7wQ—Balicki DVD; ad17nEj_TBg, EFW6GrwDVEU): pak = slap (slap-pin), lop = pull; choose by pressure: opponent's hand crosses your centerline → lop, stays inside → pak. Lop sao—“car crash: he flies into me while I hit into him”; wedge up UNDER the arm; woo sao—“why use two hands for one block?” 5 pak-sao lines: over the bridge / split entry / low / jao sao / gua choy. Torso turn essential—otherwise both remain on firing line 50/50.
- **Cycle drills and chi-sao rolling** (XpO, EFW, St8jiC_0NFY): hypnotic metronomic forearm rolling (poon sao: tan/fook/bong) ~1 beat/sec → sudden rhythm break on ANY beat—pull + strike—return to cycle. “When you feel something, you move”—tactile rather than visual reaction, “no thinking.” Loop → break → loop pattern—the best rhythmic template for trapping scenes; at speed, lop really jerks partner's head (whiplash).
- **“No blocks—parry+hit in one beat”** (fOFHFta8aFw): JKD—“assault art,” “in all actuality, there is no blocking”; block = “rigid stop”—instead divert the energy. Ideal—single beat: “My block is actually the arm. It's the hit”—your centerline straight itself diverts opponent's strike with forearm; parry-then-hit (“one-two motion”) already a compromise. Parry amplitude 10–15 cm, all within body's silhouette.
- Trapping is an attribute drill, “like skipping rope”: not forced in combat (XpO, Inosanto analogy); hand-contact reference point—“nobody fights like that except Enter the Dragon.”

## 4. Interceptions and counters

- **Jeet juk tek—intercepting side kick** (wJjnmZjwTBM): against front/round kick—NO chamber: leg simply rises, foot turns flat, torso leans as counterweight (“shift, lean, and stiff-leg”); contact in FIRST third of opponent's kick—attacker “steps onto an invisible stair” and stops. Hard version—through knee, opponent folds. Hop in 10–15 cm for height difference. Immediate stance reset.
- **Stop-kick interception logic**: “to reach me you must move to me—your attack offered me an opportunity to intercept” (_naZzjLzVXk); low intercepting knee/shin kick as “stop signal” on entry (Pa2YV2zcfNQ).
- **Leg cut vs round kick** (M8f5o-mg6ic): counterintuitive—step INSIDE round-kick arc (“your logic tells you not to do it”), shin wedges into support line—kicker is not “hit” but loses structure and collapses himself. Movie beat: villain throws spectacular round kick—hero takes tiny inward step, villain falls.
- **Kick evasion Toy Ma** (g7OInuomm2s): “retreating horse”—front leg whips behind rear, torso in profile, silhouette collapses in place—kick passes 10–15 cm from abdomen; hand counter starts BEFORE attacker's foot touches floor (“stolen beat”).
- **Sidekick counters** (9xs25_whwcg, Balicki): ha pak (low slap “like dribbling” → ricocheting eye jab), jeet que (forearm “shock absorber”: strong pressure—spring back, weak—stand), jik tek (rebound + retreating toe kick to groin—“hit—opponent roots in place”). Round kick taken with shoulder roll (“roll with the punches”).
- **Entries / bridging the gap** (Pa2YV2zcfNQ, 9xs, AWH): entry is a low sliding lunge rather than jump: “spear” hand before foot, bridge from kicking range to trapping in 1 beat; eye jab (bui jee) as opener—“be Houdini, make him believe you really are reaching for his face”; ABD—“throw a FULL strike in the feint, not half.” Range flow as movie combination: kick range → trapping → boxing → takedown/lock (_naZzjLzVXk—explicitly says “that could be used in film”); ground finishes: jao sao → takedown → armbar at 45° (9xs).

## 5. What JKD TRAINING looks like (training-scene reference)

- **Mitts/targets**: focus mitt as “responding hand”—mitt trapping looks like combat, not fitness (XpO: block knocked SIDEWAYS, cross-hook-cross-lop progressions; 3-minute rounds: slow → faster → “go-go-go”); small round mitt with ringing snaps (7M3); kicking paddles, holder retreats—attacker pursues with pendulum entries (Clf); small white speed target (5OZ); bean bag timed to “not yet… not yet” (_na).
- **Wooden dummy**: lop-sao power test—“if you can move the dummy—you can move a person” (ad1).
- **Isometric straps, tendon power** (t08AT17B_ks): Lee's 5 exercises—pull strap from beneath foot in 3 arm-extension phases (ready striking phases for storyboard), vertical-fist pushups with holds and “explode up,” dumbbells in extended arms, press against fixed bar (pose from famous Lee photo). Dramatic structure: static tension trembling for 10–20 sec → explosion. “It's tendon power… not just muscles.”
- **Clap/auditory drills** (cQ-, z1a): strike/step on clap from dead stillness, clapper outside field of view—“blind reaction” staging; broken-rhythm commands.
- **Rolling** (St8, EFW, XpO): paired hand rolling—“meditation → flash”; feet together, all drama in hands; best angle—strict profile, hands silhouetted.
- **Master-student staging**: coach circles, manually adjusts feet/torso, white floor tape as alignment axis (wmF); commands “Relax. Relax. Relax” (cQ-); Ted Wong makes tiny foot-angle corrections on hotel carpet (OgG); female instructor strikes larger man—mechanics from structure, not strength (XpO); sneakers worn—“art for the street” (9xs).
- **Setting palette**: London rooftop + dark gym with Lee posters (5OZ), wooden DVD studio 2004 (XpO/9xs), loft with panorama skyline (RTu/Pa2), penthouse (aqn), fenced backyard (ad1/EFW), Thai bag-filled gym—found-footage seminar (M8f), VHS conference room 1998 (OgG), yin-yang puzzle mats (Vxm), archival b/w 8mm—Lee's backyard with Coburn (_na).

## 6. Street logic

- **3 street-fight mistakes** (aqn6cy3jB0k): (1) “Thinking It's Going To Be A Fair Fight”—sucker punch, hidden knife, no rules; (2) “Giving The Attacker The Signal That You Want To Fight”—raised fists = escalation; JKD alternative: deceptive neutral pose, open palms “I don't want trouble,” weight already on rear leg—an invisible first strike comes from it; (3) “Overestimating The Time And Distance”—aggressor 1.5–2 steps away closes gap in one lunge faster than reaction.
- JKD is self-defense, not sport: “we wanna finish it in seconds” (z1a); “this isn't sport—it's street fighting”: eye wipe, palm to ear, hand to throat (XpO); longer exchange (“one, two, three”) means greater risk (fOF); cup and partner caution mark “dirty” targets: groin, eyes, throat, knee.
- Setting contrast for scenes: “one word leads to another” conflict against sophisticated surroundings (glass, harbor panorama), “lull → burst” rhythm—1-second action flashes within long static dialogues (aqn).

## 7. Timing / tempo for animators

- **Basic rhythmic formula of all videos**: steady cycle/stillness → sudden break → 0.5–1 sec explosion → reset/recovery. Trapping drill ~1 beat/sec, chi-sao rolling 2–3 cycles/sec, explosion on unpredictable beat (EFW, St8, Vxm).
- **Within a strike**: retract faster than extension; drop the weight on exact impact frame; chain punches—3–5 consecutive strikes after each entry, then stance recovery (5OZ); “one, boom”—defense and attack in one beat, no “one-two” (fOF, AWH).
- **Sparring tempo**: short 1–2-second explosive series with recovery pauses (5OZ); ping-pong counter-recounter drill—continuous medium-speed “conversation of strikes,” template for long “dialogue” scene between equals; “not about speed—about cadence and rapport” (9xs).
- **Interceptions steal a beat**: counterattack begins before opponent completes attack—stop-kick in first third of opponent's kick (wJj), counter-hand before foot lands (g7O), entry before halfway through round-kick arc (M8f).
- **Hidden loading**: pendulum—outwardly static, fires without preparation (wmF); sound-triggered start from dead stillness (cQ-); broken rhythm—unpredictable intervals (z1a).
- **Training speed levels**: slow/clean → normal → lightning (“easy to mask mistakes with speed—slow down,” 9xs); heavy backfist—the only “wide” frame with full commitment and following pause (7M3).
- **Editing contrast**: long meditative monologues vs 1–2-second archival explosions (_na)—trailer rhythm model.
