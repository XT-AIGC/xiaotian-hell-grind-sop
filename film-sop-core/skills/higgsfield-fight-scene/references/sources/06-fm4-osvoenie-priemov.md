# 06 — Bruce Lee's Fighting Method, vol. 4: Mastering Techniques

**Type:** primary-source book (scan, photo sequences; Russian edition “Zdorovye Naroda,” Moscow, 1994; original Bruce Lee's Fighting Method — Self-Defense Techniques, Ohara 1976)
**Important:** in the Russian division, “book 4” = the applied self-defense volume. Its content is street-defense scenarios—surprise attacks, grabs, chokes, weapons, group combat—not sparring or the five ways of attack. This is the series' main volume for “dirty” street choreography.
**What it adds to choreography:**
- Ready 3–9-phase photographic sequences “stimulus → counterattack → finish” for street scenes (not duels).
- Lee's signature logic: do NOT block, but seize initiative—a counterstrike during the opponent's windup (interception = stop-hit).
- Victim reactions specified: loss of balance, falling sideways, toppling—useful for physics prompts.
- Scenario settings: street, automobile, wall, lying down, two or three attackers.
- Psychology: a “telegraphed” windup as counterattack window—the key to editing timing.

## Lee's principles from this volume (tactical framework)
- Best defense against a surprise attack is alertness: “always remain aware of your surroundings”; attacker must not catch you off guard.
- “The best defense is to be slightly faster than the attacker.” Execute every technique accurately, powerfully, quickly.
- Against windup/grab—not a block but immediate counterstrike (fingers to eyes, foot to knee/groin): opponent “telegraphs” the strike, creating a window.
- Low side kick to knee/shin—Lee's favorite universal answer; practiced on a heavy bag (~35 kg).
- Never turn your back to an opponent (exception—instant turn with an elbow strike).
- In confined space (a close hold), only hands/elbows/knees work; feet require distance.
- Against weapons: stick “telegraphs” more than knife; pistol is most dangerous; best technique is avoiding confrontation.

## Counterattack sequences: surprise attack (ch. 1)
- **Attack from the side in the street:** Lee notices someone by wall → moves away early, maintains distance → as opponent lunges: side kick to supporting knee → opponent loses balance, falls sideways → straight and cross punches to face until knocked to ground.
- **Attack while entering a car:** Lee, half seated in car, sees a kick windup → preempts: side kick to attacker's knee → opponent knocked to ground.
- **Pursuit from behind:** Lee senses a “tail” → before opponent punches: side/back kick to knee → turn → finishing round kicks to sinking opponent's face. Comment: an early turn would let opponent prepare—counterattacking from a “blind” pose gains a beat.
- **Attack by car (kick to abdomen):** step back from kick → side kick to shin/knee → hand strikes to falling opponent's face.

## Unarmed opponent: strikes (ch. 2)
- **Against swing:** from left-lead stance, side kick to lead knee → collar grab → knuckle strike to face.
- **Against straight punch (overhead view):** simultaneously with opponent's step—right kick to groin. Lee: “a block before a counterstrike is an extra beat; immediate counterstrike is more effective, but you must be faster.”
- **Against rear-hand cross:** opponent “opens up” → weight onto left leg → right kick to chest before his fist arrives.
- **Against hook 1:** forearm block by temple → immediate finger jab to eyes.
- **Against hook 2 (no block):** clockwise torso turn + weight transfer → extended finger strike to eyes—arm length and displacement move head off strike line.
- **Against hook 3:** turn side-on, weight onto left leg → right kick to abdomen. Comment: windup “telegraphs,” providing sufficient time; Lee himself preferred fingers to eyes.
- **Against leg takedown 1:** step back → front kick to diving opponent's face.
- **Against leg takedown 2:** step back → grab hair and arm → topple, turn onto back → kick.
- **Against leg takedown 3:** step left foot back, solid base → grasp head with both hands → step right foot back, press head down until fall → hold.

## Unarmed opponent: grabs (ch. 3)
- **Chest grab + windup:** block left → catch his arm → right fist to face → leg reap, throw to ground → hop away from his leg → heel strike to grounded face.
- **Two-handed arm grab (1):** turn toward opponent → low round kick to knee → left fist to face → knee to groin.
- **Arm grab (2):** without extra movement—side kick to knee.
- **Wrist grab (3):** before his strike—left cross to face. Lee: a simple counterstrike is more useful than complex escape techniques.
- **Grab and inward pull:** slip forward-right away from fist → left fingers to eyes.
- **Rear wrist grab / arm twist:** sharp clockwise turn with elbow to face (exception to “never turn your back”—at speed).
- **Rear half nelson:** rotate toward trapped arm (counterclockwise) → off-balance → pin his arm with body weight → backward elbow to body.
- **Arm twisted behind back:** response—back kick to groin; if opponent stands sideways, establish distance first.
- **Two-handed chest grab (1):** left hand covers groin in advance → raise left, pinning his arms to your body → right cross to face.
- **(2):** same preparation → right elbow to face. Key: trap his hands before he strikes.
- **Rear collar grab:** small backward step, torso turn → left elbow to face (rotation powers strike).
- **Elbow lock (“lever”):** act before being toppled—clockwise turn + backward kick.

## Chokes and body holds (ch. 4)
Lee's framework: simplest responses to chokes; elbows, head, groin strikes work.
- **Front choke 1:** grab elbows (relieve neck pressure) → fingers to eyes → knee to groin; control his right leg with your right to prevent knee strike.
- **Front choke against wall:** side step → downward groin strike.
- **Headlock 1:** two fingers to eyes with free hand.
- **Headlock 2:** turn head out → upward strike to face.
- **Headlock 3:** turn torso into line with opponent → free-hand groin strike. Do not delay—he will take you down.
- **Rear choke with arm trapped:** turn right until balance breaks → left elbow backward to ribs. Key—prevent arm being secured.
- **Rear bear hug (arms pinned):** right step forward + abrupt drop down (slipping out) → left strike to groin. Movement sequence takes fractions of a second.
- **Rear body hold (arms free):** immediate backward elbow to face with torso turn.
- **Front body hold with lift:** swing pelvis → knee upward to groin.
- **Rear body hold with lift:** swing pelvis → hands down → grab genitals. Being lifted from behind is the most dangerous position; act instantly.
- **Front headlock:** before being toppled—fist to groin.

## Armed opponent (ch. 5)
Lee's position: “An unarmed person is in enormous danger; best to avoid such confrontations.” Against stick, timing and distance decide; stick and knife “telegraph,” pistol does not.
- **Stick windup 1:** retreat outside range → on missed swing, side kick to torso.
- **Stick windup 2:** squat below trajectory → grab arms → pull down → fist to face.
- **Stick thrust to abdomen:** block + tiny lateral evasion → fingers to eyes.
- **Long stick windup:** explosive closing (jump close) → left blocks arm + right fist to abdomen → hold arm, round kick → opponent grounded → fist and kick to ribs (combination finish).
- **Long stick overhead windup:** duck under stick → round kick to groin.
- **Long stick evasion 1:** retreat → forward burst → round kick to head.
- **Evasion 2:** retreat → hop → spinning round kick to face.
- **Long stick thrust 1:** move off line + grab stick with hands → hold stick, side kick to chest.
- **Thrust 2:** evade + grab stick → downward kick onto hand (disarm).
- **Knife, shirt grab:** catch his left arm → sweeping right to face → right leg reaps supporting leg from behind → on ground—heel to torso.
- **Knife thrust 1:** finger feint to face (force reaction) → at that moment kick shin, maintaining distance from blade.
- **Knife thrust 2:** round kick to wrist—knock knife away.
- **Knife slash:** step back from cutting motion → as it misses—enter and side kick shin/knee, topple.
- **Pistol in front:** forward step + sideways turn off line of fire + grab arm near elbow → free hand takes weapon → elbow lever → strike face.
- **Pistol at back:** counterclockwise turn + grab his arm (off line of fire) → left grips elbow → right fingers to throat → series of head punches. Comment: disarming a pistol is possible only at close range; there will be no second attempt.

## Group attacks and fighting on the ground (ch. 6)
Framework: against a group—see everyone, work equally with both hands and legs; someone held by two is nearly defeated, so explode first.
- **Front and rear (A holds arm behind back, B punches face):** squat beneath B's punch → turn right, free arm → wrist strike to A's ribs → right fingers to B's throat → high kick to A's head. (Classic “triple” for a 1-versus-2 scene.)
- **Two pin against wall:** kick A's groin + simultaneously block B's fist with left hand → right cross + front kick to B's groin.
- **Lying down, two on either side:** hand blocks B's leg + simultaneous bracing kick to A's knee (A falls) → hold A's leg, front kick to groin.
- **Stopped in street (second approaches):** grab B's elbow → fingers to eyes → cross to chin, B grounded → meet running A with round kick to chest.
- **A holds from behind, B attacks front:** left front kick to B's groin → hook left leg behind A's leg → grab his arms + torso turn → throw A to ground → finish with face strikes.
- **Lying down, opponent holds torso (arms free):** right hand grabs ear → left elbow to body.
- **Lying down, opponent sits on chest and chokes:** one hand loosens hold → other fingers to eyes. Comment: on ground, arsenal sharply limited, speed reduced—you cannot defend against a group from ground position.
- **Lying down, arm held while heel attacks:** sharp turn toward opponent → topple him → left fist to side.

## Choreographic conclusions for video generation
- Almost every sequence: (1) threat/grab established → (2) defensive microaction (slip/squat/step/interception) → (3) low counterstrike (knee/shin/groin) or eyes/throat → (4) opponent loses balance → (5) hand/leg finish against falling opponent. 3–5 beats, 2–4 seconds of screen time.
- Repeated counterattack targets: knee, shin, groin, eyes, throat, ribs—a “nonsporting” set distinguishing street scene from tournament.
- Reactions: knee strike—leg buckles, sideways fall; fingers to eyes—head jerks back, hands to face; elbow to ribs—folding; supporting-leg reap—fall onto back.
- Cinematic weapon pattern: feint → missed swing → explosive closing; stick provides wide readable arcs, knife short thrusts and off-line evasions.
- In 1-versus-2 scenes: defender always “deals with” opponents sequentially through crouching/turning, never standing statically between them.
