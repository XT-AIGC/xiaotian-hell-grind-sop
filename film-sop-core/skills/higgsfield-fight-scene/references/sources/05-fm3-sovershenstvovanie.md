# 05 — Bruce Lee's Fighting Method, vol. 3: Refining Technique

**Type:** primary-source book (scan, photo sequences; Bruce Lee's Fighting Method — Advanced Techniques, Lee/Uyehara, 1977, Russian translation)
**Contents:** chapters “Hand Attacking Techniques” (2 parts), “Attacking with the Legs,” “Defense and Counterattack,” “Attributes and Tactics” + photographic inserts (photos 1–190; scan captions nearly illegible, mechanics reconstructed from text).

**What it adds to choreography:**
- “Feint → opening → strike” logic: every JKD master's attack has two beats, showing one line and striking another.
- A complete set of counterattack sequences “opponent's attack → interception/deflection → counterstrike,” specifying hands, legs, and targets.
- Stop-hits as Lee's signature dynamic: the opponent's attack dies halfway, met by an eye jab or knee/chest kick.
- Range tactics: which strike to stage at long/middle/close range and how a character “reads” the opponent before attacking.
- Movement clarity rule: no “telegraph”—ideal for staging lightning-fast, sudden strikes on screen.

Basic stance for all sequences: right-lead on-guard (right hand and leg forward—the leads; left to the rear, covering). “Opposite stance” = left-handed opponent (left forward).

## Parries and defenses

- General principle: a block/deflection almost never exists by itself—it transitions into a counterstrike simultaneously or immediately afterward. Diverting the opponent's strike unbalances him and opens a line for response.
- Four responses to an opponent's face jab (from right lead):
  1. Rear (left) hand open and slightly higher than usual; as the jab comes—slight left slip, left palm knocks striking arm downward (strike to joints/forearm), then quick counter to face or body.
  2. Slip torso left + step right foot forward + powerful right fist to face/body.
  3. Turn torso right, step right foot forward, stop-hit with left fist to face or body.
  4. Step backward—the jab falls short; counter as opponent retracts his arm.
- Grabbing/trapping (immobilization): one hand pins/diverts opponent's arm, the other strikes. Wrist grab with inward pull + simultaneous counterstrike; stepping on opponent's extended front foot removes his kick and step (then palm strike, left hand controlling wrist).
- After any block: move opponent's arm away and strike while he straightens/recoils and his feet are stationary. After a grab, keep pressure—immediately strike again, denying recovery.
- Defense against knee/shin low kick: do not retreat straight back—move sideways, check the kicking leg with a meeting motion, or break range in advance.
- Against double strikes: first is deception, second real; maintain the block through the entire series.

## Evasions and body movement

- Left/right slip with simultaneous counterstrike—the foundation of hand counters (left slip → right fist; right turn → left fist).
- On a body strike (left cross/downward hook): torso bent, duck down to strike level, chin against striking arm's shoulder, head tucked—shoulder shields jaw from counter.
- On backfists and lateral strikes, torso leans opposite the striking hand—for balance and to move head off the line of fire.
- Backward step with torso lean—evade jab and immediately answer with side kick (photos 156–157): torso upright or leaning back, hands ready to defend.
- A creeping, crouched opponent is harder to hit—retreat, draw him into a sweeping advance, evade lead hand, and counter with a kick.
- Pivot on ball of front foot (heel raised)—basic mechanics for body commitment: hips turn through, weight transfers forward; strengthens lead punch, hook, and backfist (+5–8 cm reach without loss of power).

## Feints

- JKD has almost no “pure” direct attacks: nearly all strikes follow deception or serve as counters. Simple attack works only against a slower opponent; an equal is opened by feinting.
- A feint must look like a real strike: presentation long and deep, real strike short and sharp (“long-short”). Deliver the feint while opponent is NOT already prepared to defend; it must force a specific defensive motion.
- Single feint against a slow or tired opponent; double feint against a fast one (show-show-hit).
- Classic “show → hit” pairs:
  - circular hand motion toward body's midline → opponent lowers hand → finger jab to eyes over lowered defense (photos 4–6);
  - deceptive right-lead gesture → opponent raises/opens hand → strike into opened line (photos 7–9);
  - series of head strikes conditions high defense → forward step + body hook;
  - false rear-hand windup before face → opponent throws hands up → left cross to abdomen (photos 48–50);
  - show low kick to knee → opponent's hands drop → high hook kick to face (photos 93–95);
  - upward hand feint → opponent does not react → change level: low side kick to knee, finish to face (photos 90–92);
  - raise rear hand → instant turn → spinning kick to face/abdomen (photos 106–113).
- A feint also consumes distance: first feint closes half the gap, strike covers the second half. Feints short, not prolonged; stay just ahead of the opponent's response.
- First and last strikes in a three-strike series share one target: body-head-body (middle strike “sells” a false level change).
- Eyes do not reveal target: when striking shin/knee, Lee looks at opponent's face, not target—concealing intent and applying psychological pressure.
- No telegraphing: no hand withdrawal, shoulder movement, or snarl before striking—attack starts from a static stance without windup (itself a JKD visual signature).

## Counterattacks (sequences)

Format: trigger → step-by-step actions (limb → technique → target).

1. **Stop-jab to eyes** (photos 121–125): opponent winds up either hand → right lead hand (shortest path) → finger jab straight to face/eyes; left palm simultaneously blocks/covers his striking arm.
2. **Stop-punch against swing** (photos 126–128): opponent prepares a wide right swing → slip/lean forward → right straight fist to face, attack extinguished at inception.
3. **Stop-punch against left-hander's windup** (photos 129–130): left-hander draws arm back → instant lead straight to head.
4. **Stop-kick to knee** (photos 132–133): opponent begins forward burst → lift lead leg → low side kick with foot edge to knee/shin → opponent topples before reaching hand range.
5. **Stop-kick against airborne leg** (photos 134–136): left-hander raises rear leg to kick → meeting lift of lead leg → sole stops his thigh/shin in midair. Stop-hit need not finish him—suppressing the attack is enough.
6. **Side kick to chest/ribs** (photos 145–151): opponent at long range starts preparation/burst → sliding step → powerful lead side kick to chest or ribs → opponent thrown backward/to ground (JKD's strongest strike, “one is enough”).
7. **Evade + side kick** (photos 156–157): opponent's jab → backward step and lean (jab misses) → immediate side kick to torso.
8. **Double hook kick** (photos 160–163): opponent rushes forward → pivot on left leg with weight transfer (off the line) → hook kick to face, doubled if necessary.
9. **Block + hook kick to groin** (photos 164–166): right feint to face → opponent answers with right hand → block diverting arm sideways → hook kick to groin (small target, almost impossible to block).
10. **Meeting spinning kick** (photos 106–113): aggressor charges forward → quick turn on front foot, back toward him (eyes retain target) → rear leg whips into face or abdomen → opponent flies to ground. Only against charging/inattentive opponents: during turn, your back faces the fight.
11. **Backfist from a grab** (photos 70–72): opponent grabs/pins your lead arm or has just parried jab → whip arm: relaxed hand through vertical arc → knuckles to face/temple; torso leans opposite.
12. **Counter-combination after deflection:** parry opponent's arm → left foot steps forward toward his rear leg (close exit) → left fist to face/body.
13. **Uppercut against a “bull”** (low head, swinging arms): left hand diverts his right → right uppercut to chin; on a left uppercut, right hand covers face. Weight on left leg when punching right (and vice versa). Uppercut does not work against a fast, upright fighter.

Principle behind every sequence: the counterattacker is almost invulnerable—the opponent is open during his own attack and cannot defend. Best interception moment: opponent steps, transfers weight forward, finishes a technique, or loses balance.

## Distance and timing

- **Long range:** finger jab (longest weapon), low knee/shin kick (keeps puncher outside his range), sliding-step side kick, spinning kick into a rush. At long range opponent needs a complex entry plan—that gives time to counter.
- **Middle range:** hook kick (fast, variable: head/body/low), side kick, backfist with lateral shift, circular hand strike. Principal exchange zone.
- **Close range:** short whip punch (do not clench fist until contact), upward palm to chin/nose, uppercut, body backfist, hand trapping. Body strikes are advantageous: target larger and nearer.
- Attack timing: strike when opponent is unready—on a preparatory step, position change, weight shift, or when attention/perception is occupied. Attack half a beat before readiness.
- Broken rhythm: tempo change disrupts defense—slow then explode; a pause/stop before attack breaks opponent's defensive rhythm. Experienced fighter's rhythm is unreadable; novice cannot keep up.
- Speed comprises perception (eye catches moment), decision selection, movement initiation, execution, and direction change. Tense muscles steal speed: relaxation until contact = sharpness and accuracy.
- Reading opponent (specified as a fight stage): during preparation study habits, stance, favorite techniques, reactions to feints. Mechanical fighter repeats; intelligent fighter adapts tactics to opponent.
- Countertactics (rule of opposites): against stop-hit specialist—counterattacks; against feinter—stop-hits; against hand fighter—legs; against kicker—hands; draw out the impatient and suppress with a last-moment stop-hit.
- Size: short fighter rushes close (his strength)—keep him at maximum range and exhaust him; tall fighter has long arms but is slow—remain out of reach and enter with an explosive closing movement. Against aggressor, do not backpedal straight—meet and parry, disrupting balance.
- Gaze: long range, look into opponent's eyes; close combat, at upper torso; never at your own target.
- Simple before complex: begin with simple techniques, use complex ones only if simple ones fail. A quick simple strike works especially well after complex preparations—it is unexpected.
- Provocation as story beat: deliberately expose a body part (bait), wait for attack there, punish with a preselected counterstrike.
