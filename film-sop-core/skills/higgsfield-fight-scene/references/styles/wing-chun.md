## How Wing Chun Differs from JKD ON SCREEN (Critical for Prompts)

| | JKD | WING CHUN |
|---|---|---|
| Stance | sideways, strong side forward | SQUARE-ON, narrow, knees turned inward |
| Range | entry-exit, 4 ranges | sticks close and does not release |
| Rhythm | broken rhythm, burst-stillness | continuous pressure, a “sewing machine” |
| Strikes | whips and pistons with a step | short pistons from the chest, elbows tucked |
| Legs | side kick with the outer edge of the foot | straight forward stomp with the heel/whole foot |
| Victory | 2–4 beats, interception | tying up the hands → a series until collapse |

## Snippets (Seedance-proof: Geometry, Zero Terminology, Zero Prohibitions)

**WC-S1. Stance (Yee Ji Kim Yeung Ma)** — Wing Chun stance, Ip Man's stance
`He faces the opponent SQUARE-ON, feet narrow — barely wider than his hips — toes turned slightly inward, knees pressed toward each other, weight sunk low in the pelvis. Both hands float stacked on the vertical line down the middle of his chest: one arm half-extended, fingertips forward, the other folded behind it at the sternum. Elbows hang heavy and close to the ribs. His face is still, chin level.`

**WC-S2. Man Sao / Wu Sao (Working Frame)** — probing lead hand
`His lead arm floats half-extended down the center, fingertips questing like an antenna; the rear palm waits open beside his sternum — a two-layer fence guarding one vertical line.`

**WC-F1. Shifting Step (Juen Ma)** — pivoting in place
`His feet stay planted as his whole body pivots a quarter turn on the heels, the frontal frame swinging like a door on its hinge — the punch slides past his chest a palm's width away.`

**WC-F2. Pressing Step** — advancing step
`He shuffles forward in small flat steps, rear foot pushing, front foot skimming — each step half a shoe long — walking INTO the opponent, never letting a gap open.`

**WC-H1. Chain Punches** — chains, “sewing machine” (= H7, Wing Chun execution)
`Both fists take turns pistoning from the center of his chest down one straight rail — vertical knuckles, elbows brushing his own ribs, each fist returning to the sternum as the other fires, three-four hits a second — while his flat steps eat the space. The opponent's head and chest rattle backward under the drumroll until his back slams the wall.`

**WC-K1. Straight Stomping Kick** — front stomp
`His knee lifts on the center line and the flat of his foot stomps straight forward into the opponent's thigh, driving THROUGH like closing a heavy drawer with the sole. The man's hip shoots back, his frame folding at the waist.`

**WC-T9. Tan Sao (Dispersing Palm)** — tan sao
`His forearm rotates palm-up along the center line, wedging the incoming punch outward off his chest — and the rear hand fires through the same window in the same beat.`

**WC-T10. Bong Sao (Wing)** — bong sao
`Under the pressure of the blow his arm folds into a high wing — elbow rising above the wrist, forearm sloping down — the strike rolling off it like water off a roof; his body pivots with it and the freed hand answers along the center.`

**WC-T11. Fook Sao (Covering Hand)** — fook sao
`His hand rides on top of the opponent's forearm, wrist hooked over it, elbow sunk — reading every twitch through the contact, owning the line from above.`

**WC-H2. Palm-Heel Strike (Wun Jui)** — palm strike
`The heel of his palm rams straight out from his sternum into the chin, fingers up, the whole arm a piston; the head jolts back as if hitting a doorframe.`

**WC-H3. Biu Jee (Spear Fingers with Power Release)** — finger thrust
`His arm floats out soft as cloth — and in the last finger-width it stiffens like a whip cracking, fingertips spearing the throat. The man clutches his neck, choking backward.`

**WC-E1. Elbows (Kup Jan / Gwoi Jan)** — downward/sideways elbow
`At chest-to-chest range his folded arm drops a diagonal elbow across the collarbone line — a short heavy chop from the frame itself, no swing behind it, the whole body sinking with the hit.`

**WC-G1. Combining Velocities (Pull + Meeting Strike)** — pulling into the strike
`His left hand clamps the opponent's wrist and RIPS it toward himself while his right palm-heel fires forward — the man's own stumble adds to the blow, two speeds meeting in his chin.`

**WC-D1. Training Scene: Wooden Dummy** — Muk Yan Jong (details — sources/15)
`He drums around the wooden dummy's three arms in a fast steady pulse — forearms knocking wood, palms slapping, a low kick thudding into the bent leg — slap-tok-boom, the heavy post shivering in its frame, the rhythm breaking and restarting like a drummer changing patterns.`

Trapping T1–T8, Chi Sao T7, and the firm S2 frame are shared with the JKD catalog (Wing Chun is their source), performed from the square-on WC-S1 stance. Wing Chun target map: throat, eyes, solar plexus, groin, knee — all on the centerline.

## Dramaturgy of a Wing Chun Fight

The classic form (and Ip Man's cinematic code): the opponent circles and winds up — the master stands square-on, almost motionless, hands in a frame → forearms make contact → 2–3 beats of sticky restructuring (tan/bong/fook, pak/lop) → a corridor opens → chain punches until the opponent collapses → the master lowers his hands back into the frame and stands as before. The victory looks like “going through a meat grinder”: the opponent retreats under a drumroll while the master follows in small steps.

## Style Checklist (Replacements for the SKILL.md Checklist)

- A narrow SQUARE-ON stance (WC-S1) in the first frame — reprise “his narrow square-on frame”
- Sticky contact: at least one beat with the forearms connected and a readable restructuring through contact
- Chain punches (WC-H1) as the main finisher; the series accompanies the pressure steps WC-F2
- Kicks are straight stomps below the belt only (WC-K1)
- Elbows ALWAYS close to the ribs — reprise “elbows hang heavy and close to the ribs”
- FIGHT STYLE LOCK (Wing Chun version): `every kick stays below belt height; every punch is a short straight piston fired from the center of his chest, elbows close to the ribs; he stays square-on and narrow, walking forward in small flat steps; the fight is one vertical line and relentless forward pressure.`
